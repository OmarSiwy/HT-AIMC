# N3: Storage cell, weight density and weight write

2026-10-05, revised the same day after two adversarial critics (see "Critic responses"). Owner
module: `scripts/compiler/metrics/arch_eval/nodes/n3_cell.py`. Objective:
[ARCH_METRIC.md](../../ARCH_METRIC.md). Reproduce every number below with:

    nix-shell -p 'python3.withPackages(p:[p.numpy p.scipy])' --run \
      'cd scripts/compiler/metrics && python3 -m arch_eval.nodes.n3_cell --score'

Without `--score` the module runs its self-check (PASS/FAIL) and prints the per-option cell
numbers. `--regime=<name>` and `--opt=<name>` restrict the run; `--asis` adds the scores without
the real-C settling correction.

**Labels.** *measured* means simulated here with ESPice BSIM-CMG ASAP7 (TT corner; decks under
"Reproduction"). *derived* means a law applied to measured parameters. *projected* means
literature or law only. Every ARCH_METRIC number on this page is **projected**, because each one
composes projected tile constants from the other nodes with the cell model. Where an N3 input is
measured, the text says so.

## Headline (read this first)

1. **No N3 option beats the digital systolic baseline on tok/s per die in any regime.** The
   projected baseline at the current state of the evaluator is 30,806 tok/s per die, 7.53 TOPS/W,
   382.5 tok/W and 694.9 tok/J on the ARCH_METRIC die. Under Sohu conditions (1063 mm² die) it is
   62,571 / 10.82 / 71.9 / 124.4. The best N3 option reaches, under the N8-gated live stack (the
   only regime ARCH_METRIC accepts), **10,272 tok/s per die, 1.58 TOPS/W, 94 tok/W, 112 tok/J**
   (snapshot 22:33, `gaincell_mom_caps_mom6` and `gaincell_mom_caps` tied at Cu 0.5 fF). That is
   3.0× below the baseline on tok/s and 4.8× below on TOPS/W. Under Sohu conditions (live stack,
   22:40, a small grid) the same cell gives 29,778 / 7.77 / 52.5 / 90.2, which is 2.1× below the
   baseline (gaincell_mos_caps 20,463; gaincell_mos_r08 12,214). On the legacy 28 dB gate (which
   N8 now rejects) every dense option sits at 29.4k to 30.0k, below the baseline's 30.8k, and its
   TOPS/W (4.4 to 4.6) is lower too.
2. **The earlier lead claim was wrong.** "gaincell_mos_caps is best or tied-best in every regime"
   came from three errors: SRAM pushed-rule storage areas, a settling time computed with C_eq
   instead of the real gate charge, and a grid that stopped at 128 rows. With logic-rule areas from
   the ASAP7 liberty, the measured 85 C retention tail and real-C settling, the 0.7 V MOS cell
   **loses to min-pitch MOM** wherever density binds: 32.7k against 37.0k tok/s with the power cap
   lifted, and 468 against 574 at the 43 dB gate.
3. **Revised recommendation (DEFAULT plus one conditional co-lead):**
   - `gaincell_mom_caps_mom6` (**new n3 DEFAULT**): logic-rule gain-cell bits steering MOM units at
     min pitch on M2-M5 (4.78 fF/µm², a derived geometric upper bound). It is linear and its unit
     matching is lithographic (27i1), so the only open item is an extraction of the density.
   - `gaincell_mos_r08`: MOS gate units on a separate 0.8 V cap rail with the window
     [0.45, 0.8] V. When kT/C binds, C_eq doubles while the real charge barely moves, so it wins
     whenever the cap binds: 1,671 against 574 tok/s at the 43 dB gate (frozen stack). It
     **loses 3.2× in the current live stack** (3,165 against 10,272), which is power-bound and
     wants the smallest unit: a doubled C_eq per fin is then pure energy. So it is a co-lead only
     for a cap-bound stack. It carries two risks: a permanent 0.8 V DC gate bias on thin oxide (TDDB/BTI) and
     the MOS transient that has not been simulated yet.
4. **The weights are digital bits, and this contradicts ARCH_METRIC rule 2.** Rule 2 says
   "weights are stored as analog quantities (charge or voltage on a capacitor, gain cell, or
   similar)". Every viable option stores bits, which drive binary charge units. The analog-level
   cells fail at the 6σ, 85 C retention corner measured here (rows #19-#20 and the analog-level
   equations). The lead meets the letter of "no SRAM bitcells" only because a gain cell is not an
   SRAM. **This needs a user ruling**, and so does the latch question (Critic responses, item 6).

## Scoring regimes

Live snapshots: 22:19-22:31 (all options, fins ≥ 64 / Cu ≥ 2 fF; marked †) and 22:33-22:39 (six
main options, fins ≥ 16 / Cu ≥ 0.5 fF). In the 22:33 stack the optimum sits at the smallest unit
in the grid for every option (Cu 0.5 fF, 32 fins), TOPS/W is 1-1.6, and the die is power-bound.
N3's choice then reduces to the minimum energy per unit that clears the gate, which favours a
linear MOM unit at any Cu over MOS (fin-granular, and k < 1 on a 0.7 V rail). An earlier
snapshot (before 22:04, coarser grid) ranked differently (r08 11.9k > MOS 10.8k > MOM6 8.5k, all
at TOPS/W ~8-9). That spread is the strongest reason not to over-read the live column.

22:04 and 22:18, and an identical design moved from 10.8k to 444 tok/s. The live numbers are
therefore a timestamped snapshot. The frozen regimes are the stable reference for N3's own effect.

Notes on the table:

- "as-is" in the 43 dB column is the old C_eq timing. The 0.7 V MOS cells lose 2.8× when the real
  gate charge (and its S/D parasitic) settles; the 0.8 V-rail cells lose almost nothing, because
  their k ≥ 1.
- In the frozen regime the dense options are all within 3 % of each other. The 100 W cap binds and
  N6's converters take 96 % of the pass energy, so N3 barely moves TOPS/W. The frozen model is
  also non-monotone in t_pass by up to ±4 % (decode is HBM-bound and B is quantised): a slower
  real-C design sometimes scores higher. Do not rank on the frozen column.
- The 43 dB grid optimum sits at its edge (rows = 32) for every option, so those absolute numbers
  are lower bounds. The ranking between options is stable across the edge.
- **Merged as duplicates.** Unary versus binary weighting (20i5a): a binary slice is already built
  from identical units. Constant-sum differential encoding (AD-DiffAmp-1) is the N5 differential
  topology. Ternary, multilevel and mixed radix (DD-Division, DD-Arith) are N4 formats. The
  unipolar sign-row factorisation (27k4) is the n4/n5 sign-magnitude choice.
- **Refresh-free decode** (critic suggestion) is not worth a variant. A decode rewrite comes every
  V × t_pass ≥ 18 µs, but the design retention is 1.3 µs (and the nominal one 14.3 µs), so
  streaming never outruns retention and refresh runs in every phase.

## Equations (in `n3_cell.py`, units in names)

Per 4-b slice (sign-magnitude; the sign rides the ± lines), with b = 4:

- **Units:** binary uses 2^(b−1) − 1 = 7 identical units (27i1, 20i5a). C-2C uses 3(b−1) − 1 = 8
  units, and its output Thevenin capacitance is 2Cu (27i4).
- **MOM area:** BEOL = units × Cu / ρ_MOM. ρ_MOM is 2.0 fF/µm² (derived, asap7_constants), or
  4.78 fF/µm² for min-pitch M2-M5. That is 4/5 of the 5.98 fF/µm² M1-M5 geometric bound, because
  M1 is taken by the cells' local wiring.
- **MOS area:** FEOL_caps = units × fins × A_fin × ov, with A_fin = 27 × 54 nm = 0.00146 µm² and
  ov = 2 (projected layout overhead).
- **Storage area (logic rules, from the asap7sc7p5t liberty):** one transistor is
  INVx1/2 = 0.0219 µm², and a TG is 2T = 0.0437 µm².
  - 4T gain bit: 0.0875 µm².
  - Dual-rail PMOS bit (two 2T nodes plus two PMOS switches): 0.131 µm².
  - Custom latch (6T, SRAM-Vt): 0.131 µm².
  - Std-cell latch DHLx1: 0.2187 µm².
  - 6T foundry SRAM: 0.029 µm². This is the only cell allowed SRAM pushed rules.

  `FEOL_store = bits × (A_bit + n_TG × A_TG) × 1.3`, which is 1.14 µm² per W4 weight for the gain
  bit. That is 2.8× the earlier SRAM-rule 0.41 µm², which is kept as the `_sramrule` bound.
- **Cell area:** `area/weight = max(share × FEOL_store + FEOL_caps, BEOL) / share / (1 − occ)`.
  The caps are stacked over the cells. If occ ≥ 0.95, the cell reports "no tile fits".
- **MOS signal equivalence.** n2 and n5 assume a VDD_nom/2 total drive swing. MOS units must keep
  both levels in inversion, V ∈ [v_lo, rail], with the rail fixed:
  - `k = ((rail − v_lo)/(VDD_nom/2))²`, which is 0.51 for [0.45, 0.7], 1.00 for [0.45, 0.8] and
    1.65 for [0.35, 0.8];
  - `C_eq = C_sig × k × C_sig/(C_sig + C_par)`, where C_sig = fins × C̄(V) + C_MOM-over. SNR is
    Q²/(kT C_tot), so the column-side parasitic enters as C_sig/(C_sig + C_par);
  - `C_par = fins × (units+1)/units × C_j` is the S/D diffusion of every unit on the summing node,
    with C_j = 11.3 aF/fin (switch_coff, measured). That is 8 strips × 32 fins for a 7-unit
    weight: +20 % on the column, −0.8 dB (derived). It is zero for an active virtual-ground
    column (`active_col`).

  The option's `cu_fF` is set to C_eq, which is what n2's energy and n5's SNR read.
- **Real-C settling.** The column RC charges the *real* charge-carrying cap, C_real + C_mom +
  C_par, not C_eq. The cell reports `settle_c_ratio = C_w,real / C_w,eq`, which is 2.8 for
  [0.45, 0.7] and 1.4 for [0.45, 0.8]. Live n2 does not read it yet, so `score_option` re-times
  n2 by calling n2's own `array()` with the real per-weight C and taking t_word, slot and tau from
  that call. Energy and SNR still come from the C_eq call. This is exact under n2's law, unlike the
  critic's patch (×1/k on all of t_word), which also scaled the fixed sky130-anchor slot.
- **MOS unit mismatch:** `σ_u = rss(σ_Vt |C(v_lo) − C(rail)| / q̄, A_C/√(n W L))`.
  - The Vt term is derived: measured C(V) with a projected A_VT of 1.3 mV·µm. It is 0.20 % at 32
    fins.
  - The geometric term is projected: A_C = 0.15 %·µm (range 0.1-0.5) for L/LWR, H_fin and EOT. It
    is 0.69 % at 32 fins, so the total is **0.72 %**.
  - Over the plausible A_C range the total is 0.5-2.3 % at 32 fins, which straddles the 1 % kill
    line. At the 43 dB gate's 256-1024 fins it is 0.13-0.25 %.
  - n5 reads `mos_unit_sigma`. The earlier claim that "n5 is pessimistic for MOS" is withdrawn.
- **Retention:** `t_ret = ∫ C_node dV / I_node(V)` from the storage rail down by ΔV = 0.2 V, with
  C_node = 5 fins of gate (0.30 fF).
  - Nominal (TT 27 C, measured): I_node is the write FET at WWL −0.15 V plus 5 × the gate
    tunnelling. Result: **14.3 µs** at a 0.7 V storage rail (13.4 µs in the previous version; the
    leakage table now has more points) and 8.7 µs at 0.8 V.
  - **Design corner (measured): the 6σ write FET at 85 C.** The −0.20 V Vt shift is applied as a
    +0.20 V WWL proxy, giving 39-47 pA at 0.5-0.7 V against 2.0-5.9 pA nominal, with the 85 C
    D=S=0 gate leakage added. Result: **1.32 µs** (1.16 µs at 0.8 V). Refresh is sized to this
    corner, because the worst of ~1.8×10⁸ bits per die sets the uniform refresh rate.
  - Row repair, as in DRAM, could relax it to a 4-5σ cell (projected ~3×). That lever is not
    modelled.
- **Refresh:** eDRAM-style local sense and restore through the bit's own read FET:
  - `E_ref = E_wr + nodes × (½ C_RBL V² + E_SA)`;
  - `P_ref = bits × E_ref / t_ret,design`, which is 47.9 nW per weight for #4 and 43.6 for #1;
  - `occ = 2 R t_row / t_ret,design`, which is 11 % for #4, 9.3 % for #1 and 19.6 % for #5.

  Occupancy is charged as area. The previous text's "1.9 %" belonged to the MOM option at the
  nominal corner; the lead was then 0.7 %.
- **Crowbar (new, measured):** a drooping '1' half-turns-on the PMOS of the inverter that makes
  the TG complement. This term is averaged over the droop [0, ΔV], with half of the bits at '1'.
  - RVT inverter: 4.0 nW/bit, i.e. 8.0 nW per weight.
  - SRAM-Vt inverter (now specified): 0.9 nW/bit, i.e. **1.8 nW per weight**.
  - With ΔV = 0.1 V the crowbar falls to 0.08 nW per weight, but the design t_ret halves (0.62 µs)
    and occupancy doubles (23 %), so ΔV stays 0.2 V.
  - The dual-rail PMOS cell (#8) does not escape this term: its off PMOS switch sees the same
    half-on bias between the v_hi and v_lo rails.
- **Leakage per weight (#4):** 47.9 nW refresh + 1.8 nW crowbar = 49.7 nW, against 3.7 nW
  previously. Over ~4.7×10⁷ resident weights that is about 2.3 W per die (derived), 2.3 % of the
  100 W cap. It is not decisive, but no longer negligible.
- **Write:**
  - `C_WBL = R (C_j + pitch × c_wire)`, with pitch = √area;
  - gain bit: `E = nodes × (½ C_WBL V² + C_WWL (V + 0.3)²)`;
  - SRAM and latch: `E = C_WBL V² + 2 C_WWL V²`;
  - `t_row = 2 FO4 + 5 (R_on/8 + r_w L/2) C_WBL`;
  - V is the storage rail, which is the cap rail for MOS cells.

  The WWL boost (V + 0.3 = 1.0-1.1 V on thin oxide) is a reliability item (digest 18s3). The
  alternative is a negative-WWL rail, which needs a charge pump (area not modelled).
- **S-shared cap, resident (#18).** Take S weight sets per cap site and n sites per die. Then dies
  D' = need/(S n), and the time per vector is T' = S t_pass. So tok/s per die ∝ 1/(T' D') =
  n/(t_pass need), independent of S. It is neutral under per-die normalisation and costs S×
  latency. Dominated (derived).
- **C-2C versus binary under kT/C (corrected).** Equal SNR needs equal column charge variance, so
  Cu,c2c = Cu,b × 4^m / (2(2^m − 1)) for m magnitude bits. The area ratio is
  `(3m − 1) × 4^m / (2 (2^m − 1)²)`, which is **5.2 at m = 3 and 10.2 at m = 7**. It *grows* with
  bits. C-2C pays only where Cu is set by matching or minimum size, never where kT/C binds. The
  earlier "C-2C pays at ≥ 6 b" was wrong.
- **Analog-level cells (#19, #20), corrected.** `analog_copier_area()` now sweeps gm/ID ∈ {5, 8, 15}
  and takes C_S at the read FET's own gate density (~24 fF/µm², including ov = 2). A ratiometric
  reference (27d7) cancels the *common* droop, down to a residual fraction of 0.15.
  - Nominal corner: the best case (gm/ID 5, residual 0.15) holds **20 µs with 3.5 % re-copy at
    0.04 µm²**. Retention alone would not kill it here.
  - **Design corner (6σ, 85 C):** the tail leakage is local and does not cancel. Every gm/ID gives
    a hold of 1.4-1.6 µs with 45-49 % re-copy, at 0.35-0.97 µm².
  - The re-copy source is either a local digital copy (a superset of the bit cell) or an HBM
    re-fetch every ~1.5 µs.
  - The cell is current-domain: 1 % current matching needs ~0.36 µm² (6z2), and the notes reject
    the current domain (16f1). N2 scores it as `current_gaincell`.

  The verdict rests on the design-corner retention plus the re-copy source and the domain.
- **Log cell:** ΔI/I = ΔV/(n U_T), so 1 % needs ΔV = 0.26 mV, and 10 fF holds 2.2 µs nominal.
  It is worse at the design corner.

**User's notes.**

- **27i6:** a gain cell gives a non-destructive weight; retention must exceed 2^b × T_use. The bit
  form meets this with refresh. The multi-level form does not at the design corner (#19).
- **27i7:** 8T costs 1.43-1.45× and 9T about 2× the 6T area (#17).
- **27i4:** C-2C and its 2Cu output (#13, #14; corrected kT/C law above).
- **27i1, 27i3:** capacitor ratio linearity and no static current. This is why MOM is the
  default.
- **27i5:** PVT enters through C_P/C, which is the S/D parasitic term.
- **27c4, 27c8, 27c9:** levels per cell are a system property, so store bits.
- **27n2, 27l1:** E_prog/R amortisation. Under streaming the write is 0.1-0.5 % of the fetch.
- **27f3, 27f4, 27f8:** a FET write switch is an ideal selector (#28).
- **27m4, 27e2:** eNVM endurance (#23).
- **27g2:** kT/C sets the minimum capacitance per weight.
- **27n10 Q1, 27n1:** efficiency quoted at a precision that fails the gate is the classic
  unnormalised-precision distortion. This is why the 28 dB regime is labelled diagnostic.

## Interactions with other nodes

- **N2.**
  - n2 must read the cell's real charge, `c_weight_real_fF` (or `settle_c_ratio`), for settling,
    and the drive window `v_lo_V` / `v_hi_V` for delivery energy. The real delivery energy is
    ≈ C_real ΔV V_rail, about 1.9× the folded value, and the real column swing is 0.71× the
    model's. Both are negligible today, because the array is ~0.35 % of pass energy. N3's scorer
    applies the settling correction itself until n2 does.
  - Analog-level cells need N2's current domain (`current_gaincell`).
- **N5.**
  - The live `_pitch_um` already uses `sqrt(n3 area)`, so the earlier open question is withdrawn.
  - n5 reads `mos_unit_sigma`, which now includes the projected geometric term.
  - n5's `cap_nl_delta` takes the levels from `p["v_inv"]` and the *logic* vdd, not
    `cap_rail`. For the 0.8 V-rail options it therefore evaluates C(V) over the wrong window.
    n5 should use `cell["v_lo_V"]` and `cell["v_hi_V"]`.
  - MOS columns must be held within ~50 mV of ground (reset to 0, or a virtual ground). C(V) was
    measured with D = S = B = 0: a column at 0.1 V shifts the window to [0.35, 0.6] V (±8 %), and
    a mid-rail passive share drives the units into depletion.
  - The S/D junction C is nonlinear and is not in n5's cap_nl.
- **N6.** The converters are 96 % of pass energy in the frozen stack, so N3 moves TOPS/W by under
  3 %. N6 recomputes C_col from the n3 law, which stays consistent because MOS options set
  cu_fF = C_eq.
- **N8.** Under the ARCH_METRIC-valid gate (W8, 43 dB, k_sigma ≥ 32) caps grow to 256-1024 fins
  or Cu ≈ 8-32 fF. Storage FEOL then becomes secondary and the cap technology plus its *real*
  charge decide. The 0.8 V-rail MOS window is the strongest lever N3 has.
- **N1.** Writes are ≪ compute, so a single bank is right; shared-bank double buffering (#15,
  #16) helps only if N1 picks double buffering. Refresh is local and needs no HBM re-fetch. An
  analog-level cell would need a re-fetch every ~1.5 µs.
- **N9.**
  - The storage, bit-inverter and TG domain is **pinned to the cap rail** (0.7 or 0.8 V). With a
    0.5 V storage rail, an off TG's PMOS gate would sit at 0.5 V against a 0.7 V channel, the
    same half-on bias as the crowbar table (tens of nA). Only the periphery logic scales with N9,
    unless level shifters are added.
  - Refresh is sized at 85 C (6σ), and n9's `leak_scale` should not scale it again.
- **N4.** Weights must be stored as bits. W8 as two slices doubles the units per weight (14).
  C-2C never pays where kT/C binds (corrected law).
- **N7 / core.** Streamed weights travel from the HBM PHY to the tiles (~5 mm at 0.173 fF/µm, ≈
  0.2 pJ/bit, 5-7 % of the 4 pJ/bit fetch, ~1.8 W at 819 GB/s, derived). No node counts this
  term today; it belongs on the rail. A weight write is 0.08 µJ/token in the frozen score,
  against ~299 µJ/token of external-memory energy, which includes the weights plus the KV traffic
  (`external_memory` in model.py).

| MOS on a 0.7 V rail instead of a 0.8 V rail (at 43 dB) | 3.6× tok/s |
| 2 fF/µm² MOM instead of min-pitch MOM | 1.7× (no cap), 2.5× (43 dB) |

| eNVM | dead after 0.2-18 s; MRAM has no device |

## Falsifiers and simulation requests

1. **MOS-unit transient.** This decides co-lead #2. Build a 7-unit binary MOS cell (SLVT, 256
   fins/unit, gates driven 0.45 ↔ 0.8 V by TGs from dynamic bits) on a 32-128-row column held at
   ground or a virtual ground, then:
   - measure INL/DNL against the code, charge injection, and the S/D junction C(V) on the column;
   - measure the C(V) compression with the column moving ±50 mV;
   - inject L/LWR, H_fin and EOT variation as well as Vt. ASAP7 has no mismatch models, so this
     needs literature MOS-cap matching data (A_C).

   **Kill** if INL > 0.5 LSB at 4 b after a static column calibration, or if unit σ > 1 % at the
   chosen fins.
2. **Thin-oxide stress.** TDDB and BTI under a permanent 0.8 V gate bias (MOS units in
   inversion) and the 1.0-1.1 V WWL boost. **Kill #2** if the 0.8 V DC bias fails the 10-year
   target; the 0.7 V window (#4) then remains, and it is dominated by #1.
3. **Gain-cell retention transient** at 27 C and 85 C with a real TG and inverter load. Measure
   the droop of a '1' over 20 µs, one sense-restore cycle, and the crowbar during the droop.
   Monte Carlo is not available, so use the Vt-shift proxy at ±6σ. **Kill** if t_ret(85 C, 6σ) <
   0.5 µs, which would push occupancy above ~30 %.
4. **Write-path transient:** a 128-row WBL at 0.7/0.8 V with a boosted WWL. Measure t_row and
   energy against the derived 0.37-1.14 ns and 6-23 fJ/bit.
5. **MOM density and matching at ASAP7** (field solver or PEX; REQUIRED_TOOLING). This decides
   co-lead #1: **kill #1** if the extracted M2-M5 min-pitch density is below 3 fF/µm² or the
   matching at 1 fF is above 1 %.

**Disagreements and partial agreements, with evidence:**

1. **"Real-C settling: no-cap 45.5k → 31.0k, so MOS loses 1.36× to MOM" (the critic's patch).**
   The direction is right, but the patch over-corrects. It multiplied all of t_word by 1/k,
   including the fixed sky130-anchor slot of the frozen n2 (`slot = max(anchor × FO4 ratio,
   settle_tau × tau_sw)`). In the frozen stack the anchor binds: t_word = 80.3 ns against 82.1 ns
   with real C at 32 fins and R = 128 (measured with the evaluator, `cmp.py`). The exact
   correction re-runs n2's law with the real C. Under it, the as-is and real-C scores differ by
   −4 to +3 % in the frozen regimes and by up to 2.8× at 43 dB, where tau_sw binds. The critic's
   conclusion survives on other grounds: logic-rule area and the 85 C occupancy alone put the
   0.7 V MOS cell below MOM6.
2. **"Live: MOS 82, latch 71, MOM6 56 tok/s, ~370× below the baseline."** That was a snapshot of
   a node set that has since changed twice. The live stack moved from 10.8k to 444 tok/s for the
   identical design during this revision (22:04-22:18 edits to N2/N4/N5/N6/N8/N9). The ordering
   claim is what I keep. The absolute live numbers are reported with a timestamp, and the stable
   N3 comparison uses the frozen regimes.
3. **"Refresh-free decode variant".** Not added, with evidence: the design t_ret is 1.32 µs (and
   the nominal 14.3 µs) against the V × t_pass ≥ 18 µs rewrite interval, so streaming never
   refreshes the cell by itself.
4. **"MOM over MOS: +14-25 %, could be the real lead".** It is scored, and it loses. A MOM plate
   driven through the same TG sees the same restricted window (k = 0.51 at a 0.7 V rail), so its
   1.2 fF/µm² on M3-M5 adds only ~6 % to C_eq but its full real charge to the settling
   (448 against 468 at 43 dB). The upside needs a full-swing MOM driver, which is a separate
   TG pair per bit (more FEOL). On a 0.8 V rail (k = 1) the gain would be the critic's +6-8 %;
   that is not worth a co-lead.
5. **"PMOS-only / dual-rail removes the inverter and its crowbar".** It removes the inverter's
   area, but not the crowbar mechanism. The off PMOS switch's gate is a drooping '1' between two
   live rails, so it is half on exactly like the inverter's PMOS. Its 3-fin nodes also hold less
   (0.81 µs at the design corner, 16 % occupancy). Net: 1.86 µm², and it is dominated by #2 at
   43 dB (467).
6. **"The latch meets the rule only by naming".** Agreed, and escalated. My position is that a
   cross-coupled latch *is* an SRAM bitcell drawn in logic rules, so it should not be used without
   a user ruling. With the corrected areas the custom latch (2.02 µm², no refresh) ties the
   0.7 V gain cell (2.01 µm²) at 29.4k versus 29.4k frozen and 473 versus 468 at 43 dB. The rule
   therefore costs ~0-1 % on this axis.

## Reproduction

SPICE: five small DC/AC decks, run under the machine SPICE lock and a 4 GB memory cap, about 20 s
each. They use the repo's ESPice helper `analog/docs/gmid.py:_espice` with
`pdk_specs.get_pdk("asap7")` cards.

- **Storage-node leakage, first pass:** source on S; `Mw S WWL WBL 0 nmos_{sram,rvt} L=21n
  NFIN=1`; `Mr 0.35 S 0 0 nmos_rvt NFIN=2`; V_S ∈ {0.1, 0.35, 0.6}, WWL ∈ {0, −0.15, −0.3},
  WBL ∈ {0, 0.7}; 27 C and 85 C.
- **Revision deck (scratchpad `n3r2/meas.py`):**
  - write FET at WWL −0.15 and +0.05 V (the 6σ proxy), V_S 0.4-0.8 V;
  - read gate `M 0 S 0 0 nmos_rvt NFIN=4`;
  - 1-fin inverters `pmos/nmos_{rvt,sram}` at VDD 0.7, Vin 0.4-0.7 V;
  - all at 27 C and 85 C. The deck self-checks (PASS: the crowbar rises as the '1' droops).
- **C(V):** `M 0 g 0 0 nmos_{rvt,slvt}|pmos_rvt L=21n NFIN=4`, AC 1 MHz, Vg from −0.3 to 0.9 V,
  with Cgg = Im(I)/(2πf)/4.

The values are frozen in `n3_cell.py` (`I_WRITE_FET_PA`, `I_WRITE85_PA`, `I_WRITE_TAIL85_PA`,
`I_GATE*`, `CROWBAR_NA`, `C_MOS_AF_PER_FIN`). The full `--score` run takes ~40 min
single-process; `--regime=frozen` takes ~3 min.

The evaluator self-test (`python3 -m arch_eval.test_arch_eval`) passes except "default design:
four finite positive metrics", which fails because the live default design carries k_sigma 4
against N8's q_clip_sigma 32. That check failed before this revision and is not caused by N3.
