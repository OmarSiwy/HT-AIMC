# N1: System and memory

2026-10-05 (revision 2, after two critic reviews). Owner module:
`scripts/compiler/metrics/arch_eval/nodes/n1_system.py`. Objective:
[ARCH_METRIC.md](../../ARCH_METRIC.md). Reproduce every number below with:

    nix-shell -p 'python3.withPackages(p:[p.numpy p.scipy])' --run \
      'cd scripts/compiler/metrics && python3 arch_eval/nodes/n1_system.py'

**Labels.** Every number here is **projected**. The tile is the frozen shipped default
(`nodes/default/`: charge-domain 128x64 W4A8, SRAM bits switching shared MOM caps, 8-bit SAR per
8 columns, legacy 28 dB gate). Its constants are laws applied to measured sky130 anchors,
measured ASAP7 device data (gm/Id, off-current) and literature values. Rows marked *CB* apply the
closed-form continuous-batching law in `n1_system.py` to the evaluator's own tile, die and
(VDD, clock) grid. The digital baseline uses literature PE numbers (`baseline_systolic.py`,
synthesis deferred).

**Why the other nodes are frozen.** N2 to N10 are being edited while this runs. With the live
nodes the shipped tile fails N8's 44 dB gate (21.5 dB today), so every N1 option scores 0. That
is a tile property, and no N1 choice changes it. The evaluator self-test fails one check for the
same reason ("default design: four finite positive metrics"); every other check passes.
`study(base={})` scores the live nodes.

**What changed in revision 2.** CB is now throttled at the power cap instead of discarded
(which flips the Sohu verdict). The activation spill is charged. A retention limit, a
chunk-staged exposure law, a memory-interface width sweep, an SRAM-staged shadow option and a
two-law double-buffer area model are new. tok/J is now the max over both schedulers. The baseline
is scored without the d2d PHY, like the analog lead. The responses to each critic finding are in
the last section.

## Verdict in one paragraph

**Lead (A1): one die per model replica, weights streamed from its own HBM stack into
single-bank arrays (`stream_single`, the n1 DEFAULT), rewritten just in time per tile, INT8 KV
in HBM (n4 `w4a8_kv8`), continuous batching with two interleaved micro-batch groups, and no
die-to-die PHY.** 26,663 tok/s per die, 4.53 TOPS/W (145 1b-normalized), 266.7 tok/W,
278.5 tok/J.

- It is the compute ceiling R_c of this tile. N1 decides whether the die reaches R_c; it cannot
  raise it.
- **One condition is new.** The lead needs each streamed weight to hold for its chunk lifetime
  V·t_pass ≈ 107 µs. The frozen tile's SRAM bits (and the live n3 default's refreshed gain bits)
  hold indefinitely. An analog-stored weight on a 7 fF cap with an RVT access device holds only
  9.4 µs at 27 °C, and the die then drops to 19.9k (−25 %). At 85 °C it drops to 3.0k. N3 must
  give t_ret ≥ 110 µs at 85 °C (gate underdrive or a sram-Vt access device) or refresh locally.
- **A +9 % option needs a user decision.** Using 8 of the 16 HBM channels (6 mm² PHY, 410 GB/s,
  12 GB) returns 6 mm² to tiles and scores 29,163 tok/s. ARCH_METRIC calls the memory a knob
  ("one HBM3-class stack per die"), so this is a question for the user, not N1's call.
- Double buffering is never better than one bank. Against A1 it costs −52 % for analog-stored
  caps (12,809) and at best ties for digitally selected caps.
- Full residency ties on tok/s per die but needs about 500 iso-dies, and the user's streaming
  decision rules it out. Hybrid, partial double buffering, on-die KV and NVM are dominated or
  infeasible.
- Under the Sohu conditions CB also wins once it is throttled: 36,195 against lockstep's 34,649.
  One scheduler (CB) is used everywhere.

**Honest negative.** N1 cannot close the gap to the digital baseline. With identical accounting
(CB, spill charged, no d2d PHY on either) the baseline reaches 57,318 against 26,663, a 2.15x
deficit. Under Sohu it is 79,777 against 36,271 (2.2x, no spill on either). The gap is the
tile's compute density, which N2, N5 and N6 own.

|---|---|---|
| 16 bit | 228 | 43.7k |

The lead (26.7k) is far below the KV8 ceiling, which is why M1 can cut the interface in half
without binding much. KV format only becomes a king-metric lever once a better tile pushes R_c
above about 44k. Below that it is a tok/W lever: KV16 to KV8 gives +8 % tok/W.

## Equations (units in names; implemented in `n1_system.py`)

- **Compute ceiling.** R_c [tok/s] = n_tiles/(tiles_needed·t_pass) = 1,962/(916,096 x 80.3 ns)
  = 26.7k. This is the 27a2 roof P. Streaming and residency share it.
- **Double-buffer area law.** A_w(f) = R(C+chk)·max(FEOL·(1+f), BEOL·(1+f·cap_dup)), with
  R_c(f) = R_c(0)·A(0)/A(f). For analog-stored caps (cap_dup = 1) f = 1 gives 0.515x. For SRAM bits
  selecting shared caps (cap_dup = 0) FEOL rises from 0.43 to 0.87 µm², still under the 3.5 µm²
  BEOL, so the ratio is 1.000. The live n3 already models shared caps across banks
  (`banks_sharing_caps`).
- **Write exposure.** x = t_fill/(t_fill + V·t_pass).
  - Skewed just in time (lead, and what the core models): each tile is rewritten as soon as it
    has served its V vectors, so t_fill = t_load per tile and HBM only has to deliver the average
    r·w = 75 GB/s. x = 4.7e-5 at the evaluator's 39.5 ps/row (an unloaded 4 FO4), or 0.6 % at
    a DAC-settled 5 ns/row.
  - Chunk-staged (no skew): t_fill = chunk_bytes/BW = 8.04 MB/819 GB/s = 9.8 µs, so x = 9.1 %
    under CB and up to about 21 % in lockstep decode.
  - Double buffering pays only when x > 1 − A(0)/A(1) = 48.5 %. Neither case gets close.
- **Retention.** A streamed weight lives V·t_pass, so X ≤ r·t_ret/t_pass.
  - t_ret = C_w·(½ LSB)/I_off = 7 fF x 21.9 mV/I_off, with I_off measured in ASAP7
    (`asap7_constants.json`, per fin): RVT 16.4 pA (27 °C) → 9.4 µs; RVT 132 pA (85 °C) → 1.2 µs;
    sram-Vt 4.9 pA → 31 µs. A −0.2 V underdrive at 65 / 78 mV/dec (projected) multiplies these by
    1,200x / 370x.
  - The HBM budget sets the lower end of V: V ≥ w·X/(BW − kv − spill) ≈ 180 vectors (about
    14 µs). The feasible window is [~14 µs, t_ret]. Below 14 µs the die becomes HBM-bound (R1, R2).
- **Continuous batching.** X = min(R_c·(1−x), att, (BW − r·w)/(kv_tok + spill(V)),
  B_max·r·(P+G)/G, r·t_ret/t_pass), maximized over r ≥ 20.
  - kv_tok = (G·(ctx_avg + 1) + 2P)/(P+G)·kv_bytes = 7.67 MB per token at KV8.
  - Little: B_d = X·G/((P+G)·r). V = X/r.
  - Two micro-batch groups interleave: group B runs QKV on the same chunk while group A's
    attention runs on the rail. This needs V/2 = 667 vectors per group, still above the ~180 HBM
    minimum, so R_c holds. Without the interleave the compute term is 1/(1/R_c + 1/att) (A1-s).
  - **Power cap: throttle, do not discard.** X ≤ nd·(cap − static)/e_dyn per token
    (clock-gated duty cycle). Revision 1 discarded over-cap points, which made lockstep look
    better under Sohu.
- **Activation spill.** For each matrix whose V inputs overflow the buffer (V·K·a > buf), the
  cheaper loop order wins: (a) re-read the input once per K-complete chunk (K·a·c), or
  (b) block V into buffer-sized groups V_b = buf/(K·a) and re-stream that matrix's weights V/V_b
  times (K·N·b_w/8/V_b). Outputs are written once (N·a). For A1 this is 1.97 MB/token, about
  53 GB/s and −2 % tok/W. Order (a) alone gives about 5 MB/token, which matches the critics'
  estimate. The core charges no spill for any candidate.
- **KV capacity.** B_max = (HBM_bytes − w_bytes − embed_bytes)/(kv_bytes_per_token·(P+G)) =
  228 / 457 / 915 at KV16 / 8 / 4.
- **KV intensity (corrected).** Llama-3-8B has hq/hkv = 4, so each KV element read serves
  4 MACs per decode token, for any B. The bytes per token are unchanged: 64 KB of KV8 per
  context position, 37 MB per decode token at ctx 576.5.
- **Hybrid bound.** gain ≤ φ·w/step_bytes·(decode share), with φ = n_tiles/tiles_needed = 2.0e-3.
- **TP group.** d2d bytes/token = 2L·d·2 B·2(N−1)/N = 786 kB at N = 4, 3.15 µJ/token (0.1 %).
- **Resident die count.** D = ⌈916,096/1,833⌉ = 500 (467 at 1,962 tiles). ρ_W ≈ 2.65e5 per mm²,
  inside 27l8's 1e4–4e5 range.
- **Weight-write energy.** 916,096 tiles x 8,192 weights x r/X = 5.6e6 weight writes per token.
  At the evaluator's digital write that is 0.16 µJ/token. A DAC-based analog write at 0.1–1 pJ per
  weight (projected) costs 0.56–5.6 µJ/token, at most 0.15 % of the 3.75 mJ/token.

## Prefill GEMM and decode GEMV mapping (the lead)

- **Chunking.** The weights are cut into K-complete column chunks of 1,962 tiles (8.04 MB, 467
  chunks per sweep). Outputs complete inside the chunk, with no partial-sum spill (27l7).
- **Rewrite.** Skewed just in time: a tile is rewritten from a small per-tile FIFO as soon as it
  has served its V vectors. HBM delivers 75 GB/s on average, not 8 MB in a burst. The FIFO is
  N7's to size (one tile's 4 KB per in-flight tile, not a chunk-sized buffer).
- **Prefill (GEMM).** Every prompt token in the iteration's prefill share meets the chunk while it
  is written, so reuse is R = V = 1,333 (27l1: E_prog/R; 27a3: E_prog/U).
- **Decode (GEMV).** The same sweep serves all B_d = 267 decode vectors. There is one sweep per
  iteration, and r = 20 sweeps/s meets the floor.
- **Two groups.** The iteration's vectors are split into two halves that alternate between the
  tiles and the rail's attention, so the rail (11 % of a sweep) hides behind the tiles.
- **Activation staging.** Spilled through HBM at 1.97 MB/token (above). HBM total: 75 GB/s
  weights + 204 GB/s KV + 53 GB/s spill = 332 of 819 GB/s.
- **Lockstep capacity caveat.** At B = 457 a lockstep prefill holds 234k tokens of activations
  (1.9 GB residual, 6.7 GB gate/up hidden). Only 3.8 GB of HBM is free once the KV for the wave
  is resident. The lockstep rows are therefore only realizable as prefill sub-waves of at least
  114 vectors, which stay compute-bound. The core does not check this.
- **Die count and fabric.** One die per replica, no d2d PHY. A TP group (N = 2–8 on a 2.5D
  interposer) is needed only under the Sohu conditions.

## Evidence (notes read in the original; papers cited by the notes)

- **27a2.** I = B/b_w. Analog deletes only the weight-traffic term. Under ARCH (B = 250–900) that
  term is already amortized, which is why residency buys about 0 here, and why the king metric is
  compute density.
- **27a3.** E_vN − E_IMC = E_fetch/R − E_prog/U plus the arithmetic term. With streaming, R = V ≈
  10³, so the stationarity bracket survives streaming as long as the write is cheap
  (0.16–5.6 µJ/token above).
- **27l1.** E_weight = E_read + E_prog/R. "For a batch large enough that one weight set serves
  many inputs before eviction, R can stay large even with P > C. That is a serving-regime
  argument." This is the A1 regime. The retention limit (R1–R4) is the physical cap on that R
  for a charge-stored weight.
- **27l8.** Resident capacity needs P/ρ_W of area, hundreds of dies here (500).
- **27n2.** Programming is excluded only when the write happens once. Streaming pays it every
  sweep and the evaluator charges it. Write-verify (ms per core) cannot stream: C4 is infeasible
  (also 27c6, 27f8: a serial write costs N²·t_w).
- **27l2.** KV reads, not weights, bind lockstep decode (82 % of decode bytes at KV16). KV format
  is the decode lever. Analog attention is the natural fix but is out of scope.
- **27l5, 27n4, 27m2.** The wrapper and network are where system efficiency goes. N1 adds no
  wrapper: TP d2d is 0.1 % of the energy.
- **27n1.** Precision-normalized TOPS/W is reported alongside (column above).
- **27l6, 27l7.** MoE helps residency but Llama-3-8B is dense (N10). Use K-complete chunking.
- **Literature** (`ArchResearch/lit/LIT_SYSTEMS.md` §5, §7). The streamed W8/KV8 ceiling is 72k and
  the resident one 108k. SRAM-resident systems cost 100–460 iso-dies for an 8B model. Prefill is
  80 % of processed tokens, so the king metric is useful TOPS/mm².
- **KV format quality** (`LIT_QUALITY.md`). FP8/INT8 KV is lossless-tier. QServe W4A8KV4 costs
  +9.1 % PPL against +6.6 % for SpinQuant W4A8 (KV16), so KV4 adds roughly +1–3 %.
- **Repo digests.** REPO-A N1 (a)–(c): residency needs storage/compute decoupling, d2d never
  binds, KV precision is first-order. DD-FPGAs / DD-Multiplication: rewrite only where
  R ≫ T_wr/T_mvm (here 1,333/0.06 ≈ 2×10⁴). PAPER: streaming is "open for ASAP7 CMOS cells", eNVM
  residency is impossible at ASAP7, and its N1 row proposes gain-cell KV (E3, rejected on
  capacity).

## Interactions with other nodes

- **N2/N5/N6 own the king metric.** R_c caps every N1 option at 26.7k without the PHY. The gap to
  the baseline is 2.15x like for like: converters are 96 % of the weight-pass energy at the A1
  point (critic-derived from the evaluator's parts), and the weights are 94 % of the tile area.
- **N3 (cell), hard specs from N1.**
  - Retention t_ret ≥ V·t_pass ≈ 110 µs at 85 °C for an analog-stored weight, through a
    −0.2 V underdriven or sram-Vt access device, or local refresh. Otherwise the die runs at
    small V and becomes HBM-bound (R1: 19.9k at 27 °C, R2: 3.0k at 85 °C).
  - Write: under about 1 ns/row keeps exposure under 0.1 %; 5 ns/row (DAC settle) costs 0.6 %.
    The single-bank verdict survives any volatile write.
  - Reads per write: about 10³. A destructive-read cell falls to reuse 1 and the die becomes
    HBM-bound at 819 GB/s / 3.75 GB = 218 tok/s. Re-writing from on-die storage instead needs
    about 100 TB/s, so banking cannot rescue it. A sampled-copy read (the other non-destructive
    option) needs C_w/C_s > 1,333/0.03 ≈ 4×10⁴, about 9 pF per weight at a 0.2 fF sampling cap.
    That is infeasible.
- **N4/N8.** KV8: select n4 `w4a8_kv8` (it already exists), not a raw param override. KV4
  (`w4a8_kv4`) is the fallback and costs about +1–3 % PPL.
- **N7.** The d2d PHY (4 mm²) is dead area on a one-die replica: phy_d2d_mm2 = 0. The HBM PHY may
  be halved (M1, user decision). The per-tile skew FIFO replaces a chunk-sized staging buffer.
  The fixed 8 MB buffer is far too small for a 1,121 mm² Sohu die.
- **N9.** CB is throttled at the cap under Sohu: the VDD/clock choice should be read with CB.
- **N10.** Speculative decoding and MoE change B and P_tot. Neither applies to dense Llama-3-8B.
- **Core (model.py / workload.py, not N1-owned).** (1) Add CB as the scheduler. (2) Run the LM
  head only for 1 + G tokens (+5.9 % for every row; also removes about 6 % of over-counted
  useful MACs). (3) Charge the activation spill. (4) Check the activation working set against
  HBM capacity in lockstep. (5) Resident mode should size the PHY to the KV bandwidth (about
  230 GB/s per die). (6) streaming with dies > 1 scales HBM and attention but not compute or KV.

## Falsifiers (one per verdict)

- **stream_single lead.** N3's chosen weight cannot hold for about 110 µs at 85 °C without
  refresh, or cannot survive about 10³ reads per write, or the die cannot sustain a skewed
  per-tile rewrite at about 75 GB/s average from a FIFO smaller than about 1 mm².
- **double buffering dominated.** A volatile cell whose write exceeds about 0.8 µs per row but
  stays well under 100 µs per chunk.
- **resident excluded.** The streaming decision is lifted and system cost is not counted;
  resident then leads tok/W by about 1 % at an equal king metric (less after refresh).
- **hybrid dominated.** A die that holds more than 10 % of a weight copy (ρ_W 50x higher).
- **CB lead.** The core's CB implementation, with spill, comes in under the lockstep score.
- **M1.** HBM channel PHY area is not linear in channel count (a fixed PHY floor above about
  4 mm²), or the user fixes the interface at one full stack.
- **KV8 over KV4.** N8 measures KV4 at ≤ +0.5 % proxy PPL; KV4 then wins tok/W by 4 % at an
  equal tok/s.

## Critic responses

Each finding is listed with what changed. "Accepted" means the model and numbers above now
include it.

1. **Lead rests on the frozen SRAM cell, which violates ARCH #2; retention binds for an analog
   cell (missing).** Accepted. The retention limit is in `_cb_rate`, rows R1–R4 score it with
   the measured ASAP7 off-current, and N3 gets the 110 µs / 85 °C spec. One correction: the live
   n3 default (`gaincell_mos_caps`) uses refreshed digital gain bits, not an analog-stored
   weight, so the limit does not bind it. It binds an analog-level stored weight with no refresh.
2. **tok/J must be the max over both schedulers.** Accepted: 278.5 for A1.
3. **Single-group CB serializes the rail (about 24.0k).** Accepted: A1 is now explicitly the
   two-group interleave, and A1-s (23,977) is shown. V/2 = 667 is above the ~180 HBM minimum.
4. **CB discarded over-cap points instead of throttling; Sohu verdict refuted.** Accepted, and
   it was a bug. The Sohu lead is now CB at 36,195 (+4.5 % over lockstep), and "scheduler per
   condition set" is deleted. The baseline under Sohu CB with no spill is 79,777, so the Sohu gap
   is 2.2x, not 1.8x.
5. **Sohu die is 1,121 mm², not 666.** Accepted: the tables quote what was applied. The value
   drifts with n7 (1,038 mm² live today) and exceeds the reticle. Raised as an open question
   for ARCH_METRIC.
6. **Double-buffer −49 % is an artifact for the evaluated cell.** Accepted: the two-law area
   model (B1, B1-d) is in `partial_db`. The verdict is "never better than one bank" in both
   cases.
7. **Exposure 5e-5 is wrong; 8 MB in 5 ns needs 1.6 PB/s.** Partly accepted. A chunk-staged
   write is HBM-delivery bound: 9.8 µs, exposure 9.1 % (W1). **Disagree** that this is the lead's
   exposure. The lead (and the core) rewrite each tile just in time, skewed: tile i is rewritten
   when it finishes its V vectors, so the die never needs 8 MB in a burst, only the 75 GB/s
   average. Per-tile exposure is then t_load/(V·t_pass), 4.7e-5 at the evaluator's write and
   0.6 % at a 5 ns/row DAC write (W2). The falsifier is restated as a distribution-rate and FIFO
   bound, not a t_write_row bound.
8. **The 4 FO4 write is understated 10–100x.** Accepted as a sensitivity (W2: 5 ns/row,
   −0.6 %). The evaluator constant is n3's, not N1's.
9. **Prefill tokens do not need the LM head.** Accepted as a core change. A1-h shows +5.9 %. It
   applies to every row and the baseline, so the ranking is unchanged and the headline stays on
   the core's accounting until workload.py changes.
10. **Baseline gap compares no-PHY analog against a baseline with the PHY.** Accepted: the
    baseline is now also scored without the d2d PHY (57,318 under CB). The gap is 2.15x.
11. **Resident: upheld; add a refresh/PHY caveat.** Accepted. Resident dies still need about
    230 GB/s of KV bandwidth each, so removing their HBM PHY is not a free +13 %. An
    analog-stored resident weight would also need refresh against R1's 9 µs.
12. **Hybrid, eNVM, on-die SRAM KV, TP, disaggregation, activation spill order of magnitude:
    upheld.** No change beyond the spill now being charged.
13. **Analog gain-cell KV is rejected for the wrong reason (E3).** Accepted. Gain cells as dense
    digital KV storage are not an analog attention block. E3 is now rejected on capacity
    (18.4 streams per whole die at 2.5x SRAM density, against 267 needed). Analog attention
    compute stays out of scope as E3a.
14. **"KV intensity is 1" is wrong for GQA.** Accepted: 4 MACs per KV element (hq/hkv = 4). The
    bytes were already right.
15. **Destructive-read unit "sweeps/s" should be tok/s; add sampled-copy C_w/C_s > 4e4.**
    Accepted (N3 interaction).
16. **A2 is not the shipped default; KV8 is reachable through n4 `w4a8_kv8`.** Accepted. The
    shipped default is A3, 18,304. The param-ownership question narrows to phy_d2d_mm2.
17. **Missing option: right-size the memory interface.** Accepted and scored (M1). The optimum
    is 8 of 16 channels at 29,163 (+9.4 %). I scale PHY area, bandwidth and capacity together
    per channel, as the critic suggested. The critic's estimate (+3–6 % after capacity) is low
    because CB with KV8 at B_d = 171 still reaches the HBM-bound 29k. It is not made the lead
    because ARCH_METRIC fixes "one HBM3-class stack per die" as the default, and changing it is
    the user's decision.
18. **Missing option: SRAM-staged shadow next to the skewed variant.** Accepted (W3: 25,440, with
    the chunk copied SRAM-to-array at 10 TB/s, projected). It is the robust fallback if the skewed
    per-tile rewrite proves hard to route, at −4.6 %, because it pays 2.35 mm² of staging.
19. **Lockstep prefill violates HBM capacity at B = 457.** Accepted as a caveat with the numbers
    above (234k tokens, 8.6 GB of activations against 3.8 GB free). The fix (prefill sub-waves
    of at least 114 vectors) stays compute-bound, so the lockstep rows move little. It is a core
    check, listed under core requests.
20. **Missing precision-normalized TOPS/W; CB is a steady-state ideal; DAC write not priced.**
    Accepted: the 1b column, the explicit CB-vs-CB baseline comparison, and the DAC write
    energy (0.56–5.6 µJ/token, at most 0.15 %).
21. **Internal numbers belong to the PHY-kept config (V 1,245, 500 loads).** Accepted: A1 is
    V = 1,333, 467 chunks, 8.04 MB per chunk.
22. **Activation spill about 5 MB/token (order (a)).** Partly disagree. The model now takes the
    cheaper of two loop orders per matrix. For the down projection, blocking V into 585-vector
    groups and re-streaming its 29 MB of W4 weights 2.3x costs less than re-reading 14 KB of input
    four times. The result is 1.97 MB/token. Order (a) alone reproduces the critics' ~5 MB.
