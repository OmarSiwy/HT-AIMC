What changed and why:
1. **decap_under was phantom on the robust tile (critic upheld, my bug).** The slack read n3's stale
   whole-weight `beol_um2` on the FEOL-set hybrid footprint. Fixed (`_feol_slack`: footprint − FEOL). On
   the robust tile the slack is zero; the "−2,300 µm² on the robust tile" claim is withdrawn. On the fast
   (BEOL-bound) tile it stands, now 681 µm² because the plate devices take the slack first.
2. **Row drives now face the same supply law.** Bit-serial switches the same 117 pF of plates from
   VDD/GND that ml2 switches from its mid-levels. Both are now priced on the same rule: the plates settle
   to 0.1 % in one slot through the supply network, which needs **R_PDN ≤ t_slot / (R c_row ln 1000) =
   1.30 Ω per tile** (0.87 Ω at wire_x 4), with no tile-local bias. For ml2 that means V/3 and 2V/3 are two
   more die-level supply nets (bumps, top-metal mesh, die-wide decap, a die-level 3:1 ladder regulator
   whose loss is the η 0.5 delivery term already charged). Under this symmetric law **ml2 beats bit-serial
   by +11 % ARCH and +10 % Sohu** on the corner-clean tile, which flips my round-2 ranking. The open cost
   is the two extra supply nets (top-metal tracks and bumps taken from VDD/GND); the evaluator does not
   price them, so sim/extraction request S5 gates it. If S5 fails, the pick is r2_A5_bs.
3. **Row broadcast line and per-plate devices are priced.** Unrepeated Elmore 12.4 ns (1.78 mm line,
   4.6 slots); repeated optimally (9–12 INVx4) it is 2.1–2.7 ns of latency with the sample clock forwarded
   alongside, so the slot is unchanged. Plate devices (2 T bit-serial, 6 T ml2, 0.044 µm² per 2 T) sit in
   FEOL slack when there is any; on the robust tile they add 360 µm² (bit-serial) or 1,081 µm² (ml2).
   Net effect: −0.8 % tok/s, +1.3 % area (bit-serial). Projected.
4. **Comparator κ is now measured (transient noise).** ESPice `.trannoise` on the IMC StrongARM gives
   σ = 4.05 (RVT), 5.20 (LVT), 5.38 (SLVT) mV, so **κ = 1.00 / 1.73 / 1.95** (±25 %), against the derived
   1.61 / 2.59. The direction holds and the SLVT penalty is smaller. Re-scored on the fast tile: dual-Vt
   +4.0 % over all-RVT, all-LVT −12 %, all-SLVT −18 % (ARCH). **The absolute σ is 3.3x the closed form**:
   n6's comparator noise budget is likely optimistic (flagged to n6).
5. **Attribution, plainly.** The corner-clean gain over R0 (46,344) comes from removing ml2's tile-local
   bias (bit-serial, or die-level mid-rails), plus the pre-existing adc_bits 11. It is not faster multiply
   or accumulate: under ARCH the robust tile is bound by its converter (t_conv 5.37 ns) and the rail/HBM.
   **adc_bits sensitivity:** 12 b costs −25 % (43,985, power-capped at B 256); 10 b gives +5 % but fails
   corners (SS −0.72 dB).
6. **Transistors per tile.** About 82 % are in the weights: 3,145 weights × 160 T = 503k, against 16k
   (bit-serial) or 49k (ml2) plate devices, 91k in 128 SARs, and < 0.4k repeaters. **160 T per weight is
   the transistor lever, and it belongs to n3** (its 80 T column-shared variant halves it).
7. **Not evaluated:** T2 (the accumulator chain) and accumulate speed. The user's "faster accumulate" is
   still open in this node.

## Verdict (round 2 as first submitted; see the update above)

1. **Overclaim correction: the 4-level row drive (ml2_rails, round-1 V4) was free in the model and is
   not free.** The critic's gap was real. The law now charges the level buffers' settling bias (eq. 1).
   On the round-1 pick tile it costs 7.0 mW per tile, or 22 W per die. ARCH drops from 77,489 to
   **71,380** (now power-capped), and Sohu from 97,064 to **72,220 (−26 %)**.
   - V4 is not dead. A closed-loop class-AB buffer (super source follower) settles the worst-case
     59.8 pF step to 0.136 mV in the slot at **2.8 mW** (measured). The plain follower needs 22.4 mW,
     8x more.
   - V4 still loses. Bit-serial drive with no mid-level buffers beats it once the column wire is
     widened (item 2). ml2 then buys +1.8k tok/s for +20 W at the round-1 point (90 tok/s per W, the
     worst trade on the board), and at the fast point it is behind (78.3k against 82.7k).
2. **The free speed is in sizing, not in power.**
   - Widening the column wire to 4x the minimum width (`wire_x 4`, an n2 knob, no energy and no area
     because the wire is far narrower than the ~1 µm pitch) shrinks the bit-serial slot from
     1.132 ns to 0.822 ns. That gives **+15.5 %** ARCH.
   - Putting the reference decaps and the bootstrap caps under the MOM weight array (FEOL slack) cuts
     the fast tile from 18.9k to **17.9k µm²** (−5.5 %) for **+2.4 %** (now 681 µm², after the plate
     devices take their share). It does nothing on the FEOL-set robust tile (critic fix).
3. **Spending power on device speed hits the power cap, so it mostly loses.**
   - The comparator's speed comes from overdrive. At fixed noise, a lower-Vt input pair costs
     kappa x1.61 (LVT) or x2.59 (SLVT) per quiet decision (measured t_dec, derived noise).
   - All-SLVT converters double the converter energy and halve the clock under the cap: −39 % against
     the fixed lead (43,417 against 71,380), and −40 % TOPS/W.
   - The one power-for-delay trade that pays is the **dual-Vt SAR** (quiet comparators RVT, logic and
     cheap comparators SLVT). It makes t_conv 0.855x for +5 W: **+3.9 % ARCH at 626 tok/s per W**,
     −1.5 % TOPS/W, zero area. Under Sohu it is 0 %.
4. **The ceiling is close.** With an infinitely fast tile, the fast design reaches **84,769 ARCH**
   (+2.5 % above 82,669), limited by prefill attention on the rail and decode HBM. Under Sohu it
   reaches **91,573 (+0 %)**: attention and HBM bind. Any further multiply/accumulate speed must come
   with a bigger rail and more KV bandwidth (other nodes).
5. **Corners decide the pick, and my levers cannot close them for the pick tile.**
   - Every pick-family design fails the round-1 corner gate by **−2.1 dB at SS and −1.7 dB at FF**.
     Thermal and ADC kT dominate, so it is not a speed or area problem.
   - Closing them with bigger caps (cu_fF 1.75) costs 43 % of ARCH tok/s, far over the 3 % closure rule.
   - The corner-clean winner is the **robust tile with this node's levers**: bit-serial drive, decaps
     under the array, adc_bits 11, rail_headroom 1.0. Results: **61,495 / 9.65 / 568 / 568 (ARCH)**,
     **65,041 / 9.25 / 62.0 / 65.0 (Sohu)**, SS +0.65 dB and FF +0.99 dB. It sits at its own
     ceiling: the rail and HBM bind, and the tile speed does not.
   - This was `r2_A5_best.json` (now revised, see the update). Against the same robust tile with ml2
     charged honestly (46,344), it was +33 %; after the decap fix it is +27 % (58,724), and with
     wire_x 4 +33 % (61,495, r2_A5_bs).

Law (derived): a dynamic comparator's delay falls only with overdrive (lower Vt or more tail
current). Upsizing everything at the same density leaves the delay unchanged (self-loaded) and only
halves the noise.

At fixed noise, energy per quiet decision ∝ κ ∝ 1/(gm/ID)·(1/Vth). Delay therefore trades against
energy, not against area. On a die at the power cap, that trade only pays where κ is irrelevant: the
cheap (redundancy-tolerant) decisions and the logic.

## 3. Equations (units; provenance)

1. **ml2 settling bias (fix; derived from the measured anchors).** A buffer must settle the R rows
   at one level, C_lvl = R·c_row, to err inside one slot. Then gm ≥ C_lvl·ln(1/err)/t_slot (single
   pole; also the gm floor of any loop at its final small-signal end). At fixed gm/ID, P_q scales
   the same way:
   `P_q = P_anchor · (C_lvl/59.8 pF) · (1.132 ns / t_slot) · ln(1/err)/ln(1000) · (1 + x_hi)`, with
   P_anchor = 2.8 mW (SSF, measured) or 22.4 mW (follower, critic measured), and x_hi = 1.5 (SSF,
   projected 2V/3 PMOS) or 1.0. It is charged as static W per tile (`leak_W`). Area = 37 µm² (SSF)
   x scale x (1 + x_hi), from 8.5k fins at 0.0044 µm² per fin with routing. The class-AB delivery
   efficiency term (eta 0.5) is kept. On the pick: c_row 7.47 pF, R 8, slot 1.132 ns, which gives
   **7.0 mW per tile**.
2. **Dual-Vt SAR loop (`_sar_vt`; measured ratios on n6's own loop law).** n6's sar_direct is
   `t = (10q + 10)·FO4_q + (8·nd − 6q + 10)·FO4 + t_pool`. Then
   `t' = (10q+10)·FO4·r_q + (8nd−6q+10)·FO4·r_l + t_pool`, with r_q = t_dec,f/t_dec,RVT (comparator,
   measured) and r_l = FO4_f/FO4_RVT (measured; the cheap comparator measures faster still, so this
   is conservative). Converter energy changes by ×[1 + d(κ_q − 1) + (1 − d)(E_l − 1)], where d is the
   decision share of n6's breakdown. Leakage is the flavour Ioff delta on 754 fins per ADC (projected
   count), at TT 27 °C. On the pick (q 5, nd 13): dual (r_q 1, r_l 0.724) gives **t_conv 0.855x**,
   converters +2 %, and 85 µW per tile.
3. **Decap under the array (projected).** MOS decaps (reference reservoir 4·C_inj per ADC, bootstrap
   boost caps) go into the FEOL slack under the MOM weight caps:
   `slack = A_weights·(1 − feol/beol)·0.5`, where 0.5 is for M0/M1 access and a shield plane. The
   cell is FEOL 2.27 / BEOL 2.93 µm² per weight (n3), so the slack is 2,081 µm² and 1,040 µm² is moved
   per tile. Risks: lower-metal congestion, and coupling from the reference into the weight plates
   (the shield is assumed).
4. **Column slot (n2 law, sized here).** `slot = 1.2 · n_phase 3 · (B+1)ln2 · (τ_sw + τ_w) + 4 FO4`
   (ns), with τ_sw = Ron(0.7)/share_fins · C_col and τ_w = ½ (r/wire_x) L (C_col + c·√wire_x·L).
   On the pick: τ_sw 20.8 ps (16 fins), and τ_w 12.9 ps at wire_x 1 or 4.6 ps at wire_x 4. So the slot
   is 1.132 ns at wire_x 1 and 0.822 ns at wire_x 4. Raising share_fins buys more but costs Ioff hold
   droop and gate energy: at 64 fins the gate fails.
5. **Ceiling (the critic's Amdahl rule).** Scale t_pass by f in plumb. ARCH at f → 0 gives 84,769
