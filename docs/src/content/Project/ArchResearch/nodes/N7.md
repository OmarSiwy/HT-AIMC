# N7: Dataflow and scheduling

2026-10-05, revision 2 (after two adversarial critics). Decision node N7 of the architecture
search, scored against [ARCH_METRIC.md](../../ARCH_METRIC.md): tok/s per 100 mm² die (king), then
TOPS/W, tok/W, tok/J. Workload is Llama-3-8B, 512 prompt tokens plus 128 generated. The scope is
analog/mixed IMC tiles plus the digital rail. The digital systolic die is the baseline.

Model: `scripts/compiler/metrics/arch_eval/nodes/n7_dataflow.py`. It has two parts. `rail()` is
the part the core evaluator prices. `schedule()` is an analytic overlay for the schedules the core
cannot express. Reproduce with:

```sh
nix-shell -p 'python3.withPackages(p:[p.numpy p.scipy])' --run \
  'python3 scripts/compiler/metrics/arch_eval/nodes/n7_dataflow.py arch sohu'   # self-check + every row below (~2 min)
```

**Labels.** Every number here is **projected**. The tile is the frozen default (laws on the
measured sky130 anchor and on ASAP7 device data). The rail constants are literature figures or are
derived from ASAP7 constants (wire C, Ioff, DFF and NAND2 areas). The overlay rows are **derived**
from that projected tile and die. Nothing was simulated for this node. The quality harness has no
finished sweep, so N7 uses no quality number of its own; KV8 losslessness is from LIT_QUALITY.

**Context of every score.** All other nodes are pinned to their frozen defaults
(`nodes/default/`, `n7_dataflow.FROZEN`), because nodes N1 to N10 are being rewritten in parallel.
- The live default design fails the live N8 gate: effective SNR 32.9 dB against a 44.0 dB target
  (re-checked this run). It fails with any N7 option, so `test_arch_eval`'s "default design: four
  finite positive metrics" check still fails, and the cause is not N7. Every other check passes.
- The frozen tile (all values **derived** from the evaluator at the lead point): charge-domain
  128×64 W4A8, an 8-bit SAR per 8 columns, an 80.3 ns pass and 421 fJ/MAC. Converters are 98.8 %
  of pass energy (3.41 of 3.47 nJ). Density is 3.30 TMAC/s per mm² of tile and 2.17 per mm² of die.
  The die holds 2,130 tiles (65.8 mm²). PHY takes 13 mm² and 17 mm² is whitespace (tile_util 0.85,
  plus the ping-pong bank now charged).
- The systolic baseline peaks at 5.65 TMAC/s per mm² of die (W4, arch), 2.6× the IMC die, and at
  7.16 under the Sohu conditions.
- N1 is the user's streamed-weight mode with a single bank (`stream_single`). KV is 8 bits unless
  stated. The rail sizing (`rail_headroom` h as a multiple of the design's **own** peak attention
  demand, and `buffer_MB`) is free: each row is the best design reachable with that option fixed.

## Verdict

**Lead: `lean_int8_rail` with continuous batching plus a 2-group intra-step interleave**
(NanoFlow-style nano-batching: one group attends while the other uses the tiles, so the weights
stream twice per step). Also part of the lead: chunked prefill riding the weight sweeps, paged KV,
activation spill to HBM (input-re-read loop order), staggered round-robin tile reloads, the rail at
h = 2 × own demand, and a 2 MB buffer.

The headline is now the realistic **25.3k** (η = 0.85), not the 30.3k of revision 1. That 30.3k
was the tile's compute roofline: perfect overlap of three resources inside a step that the
QKV → attention → O dependency does not allow. The band is 22.6k (η = 0.7) to 29.1k (η = 1).
The serial lower bound, with no interleave, is 22.7k.

**Honest negatives.**
- At equal assumptions N7 helps the baseline at least as much as the IMC tile. The baseline goes
  from 43.4k to 50.3k (+16 %) and the IMC tile from 22.4k to 25.3k (+13 %). The gap stays at
  **2.0×** (50.3k / 25.3k).
- No schedule closes that gap. At η = 1 the lead sits at 99 % of the tile's compute roofline
  (29.1k of 28.9–29.5k). Converters are 98.8 % of pass energy, so the TOPS/W lever is N6, and the
  density lever is N2/N5/N6.
- The biggest remaining N7-owned lever is floorplan utilization, not scheduling. tile_util 0.90
  gives +7 % and 0.95 gives +14 % (the die is not power-capped at η = 0.85: 82 W of 100 W). Whether
  abutted tiles reach 0.9 or more is a layout question, so the lead keeps 0.85.

## Equations (units in names; code: `n7_dataflow.py`)

**Attention lane** (Q × K/V MAC with a 24–32 b accumulator). Multiplier area and energy scale with
the partial-product count (Multipliers notes):
- A_lane = (55 + 25·b̄·b_kv/64)·2.0 µm²; E_lane = (50 + 50·b̄·b_kv/64)·(V/0.7)² fJ, where
  b̄ = (b_q + b_pv)/2 (the mixed rail has b_pv = 16).
- Anchor: the projected ASAP7 INT8 PE of `baseline_systolic.LIT` (80 µm², 100 fJ at 1 GHz,
  0.7 V), split into multiplier and remainder. The ×2 covers operand routing, control and
  register files (27l5: the wrapper is 70.8 % of PUMA's area, but PUMA is programmable).

**Lane count, sized from the design's own demand** (`rail_demand`, `own_density`):
- lanes = h·[ (att_prefill/(P·W_mac))·ρ_die·A_die + BW_hbm·(h_q/h_kv)·8/b_kv ] / 1 GHz.
- The first term is attention's share of prefill (0.92 % for 8B at P = 512) times the design's
  peak weight-MAC density ρ_die. The study measures ρ_die per design: 2.18 TMAC/s/mm² for the IMC
  die, 5.65 for the baseline, 7.16 for the baseline under Sohu. rail() cannot see the tile, so
  the core default uses 5.7, the densest tile the rail serves.
- The second term is decode attention at the HBM KV rate. 27l2 gives I_KV = 1 MAC per element per
  query head, and GQA gives h_q/h_kv = 4.
- At the lead, h = 2 gives 10.5 TMAC/s of lanes and 2.8 mm² of rail with the sequencer. The old
  rail was 10 mm².

**Softmax.** The exponential is base 2, with log2(e)/√d_h folded into the score scale
(DD-FloPoCo+FloatingPointE #2). After max subtraction it is an 8–10 b direct ROM (Table-Based
Function Evaluation). An online (m, l, o) merge uses one reciprocal per row (Arithmetic for DL:
Softmax Unit). E_exp ≈ 100 fJ, one exp unit per 64 lanes.

**Wire, buffer and PHY energy.**
- e_w = C_wire·V²·α01·1.5 = **32 fJ/bit/mm** at 0.7 V. Derived: ORFS ASAP7 C = 0.173 fF/µm,
  α01 = 0.25 for random data, ×1.5 for repeaters. Revision 1 used 0.5 for α and got 64, about 2×
  high.
- e_in = 8(e_sram + e_w·m·pitch)/m per byte: one SRAM read multicast to m = 8 column tiles. This
  gives 61 fJ/B against 166 fJ/B unicast (m = 1).
- e_out = 8·e_w·0.5 mm + e_acc = 139 fJ/B. Blended over the core's R·a_b/8 + 2C bytes it is
  100 fJ/B; the old rail used 500.
- PHY: 0.4 pJ/bit (HBM PHY, projected) + 5 mm × e_w = 0.56 pJ/bit. The d2d PHY (4 mm²) is drawn
  only on multi-die systems. A 1 mm² host PHY is always drawn.

**Ping-pong readout.** t_pass = max(t_word, t_conv). REPO-A measured this law on sky130 (see the
critic responses). The second capture bank is now charged at 2.5 % of tile area: half the frozen
tile's ADC area, per 12j "2× the capture element". It is charged through tile_util. If the
capture is the SAR's own sampling CDAC, the cost is 0.

**NoC.**
- Router area law (DD-NetworkOnChip-1 §5): 0.70 mm² for the mesh, 0.29 mm² for TDM.
- **Link check (new).** The cluster is 0.25 mm², about 8 tiles. The cheaper of two mappings sets
  the busiest inter-cluster link: (a) the cluster holds the column tiles of one K-block (inputs
  multicast, psums chained out: 8·64·3 B per pass), or (b) it holds the K-blocks of one column
  (psums reduced in the cluster, inputs in: 8·128 B per pass).
- (b) wins at 1 KB per 80.3 ns = 12.7 GB/s on a 16 GB/s link: **80 % utilized**. Utilization above
  1 stretches t_pass. A tile 1.25× faster saturates the 128 b links; 256 b links then cost 0.8 %
  tok/s (row 22).
- Global mesh with no locality: Σ per-pass bytes/s ≤ 4√routers × link (uniform λ_max).

**Schedule overlay** (`schedule()`). With mode = lockstep, act = free and groups = 0 it reproduces
`model.system_point` exactly; the self-check asserts this, plus serial ≤ interleaved ≤ ideal.
- Tokens per weight sweep: lockstep n = B·P (prefill) and B (decode); continuous n = B(P+G)/G.
- Phase time (`_step`). Per layer, A = max(compute, weight + spill HBM) and B = max(rail, KV HBM).
  - groups 0 (the core): T = max(all). This is a roofline, not a schedule.
  - groups 1 (serial): T = A + B.
  - groups k ≥ 2 (interleaved): T = max(compute, rail, total HBM) + min(A, B)/(kL), with the weights
    swept k times and the buffer split k ways.
  - η: (1−η) of every hidden term leaks into T, in **both** lockstep and continuous.
- Residency: ws = 2d + max K·a_b/8 = 22.5 KB per token.
  - sram: ⌈n·ws/buffer⌉ sweeps.
  - spill: s_tok = Σ_m r_m[⌈N_m/(C g_m)⌉K_m + N_m]·a_b/8 + 4Ld = 5.56 MB/token (input order,
    27l7 K-complete groups g_m).
  - Output order (K-slabs, psums spilled at 3 B): 46.0 MB/token.
  - auto takes the faster of sram and spill.
- Staggered reload: when absent, each die-load (2,130 × 8192 × 0.5 B = 8.7 MB) is fetched
  unstaged and stalls max(0, W_load − buffer)/BW per load.
- Calibration: compute/(1 − cal_frac). Weight write: ×write_x.
- Sarathi budget β: 1/β of the steps carry β × the steady-state prefill, and the rest are
  decode-only.
- Per-stream floor, defined once for both schedules:
  - tbt (the core's definition): 1/the longest step.
  - avg: G divided by the request's whole residence. Lockstep uses T = T_pre + G·T_dec. Continuous
    uses (G/a + 1)·T_step.
- Speculative decoding: B(k+1) verify vectors and a accepted tokens per step.
- Exile: φ of the weight MACs move to rail lanes. They are added to the rail time term and charged
  at W4A8 lane energy (75 fJ against 100 fJ for INT8×KV8).
- Disaggregation: X = (P+G)/(P/X_pre + G/X_dec), on the lead's sizing. The prefill die has no
  per-stream floor, and X_dec is counted per die.

**Findings under the Sohu conditions.**
1. **Feasibility depends on the floor definition, and that question is the user's.** Under the
   time-between-tokens floor (the core's), IMC lockstep passes at 82.5 tok/s per stream, and
   continuous fails because each step carries 16 prefill tokens per stream (1000·17·20/8 = 42.5k
   tok/s per die needed, decode token included, against about 31.7k). Under the request-average
   floor the IMC tile fails **both** schedules: 14.9 tok/s per stream for lockstep (derived). The
   revision-1 claim "lockstep feasible, continuous infeasible" came from applying two different
   floor definitions. The baseline passes either way (27 tok/s average).
2. **The core's free activation storage overclaims by 2.6 % (IMC) at the best buffer.** The

## Corrections and overclaim warnings (revision 2)

- **Revision 1 headlined the roofline.** Its 30,291 assumed three resources overlap perfectly
  inside each step. That is impossible with streamed weights and the per-layer QKV → attention → O
  dependency unless the step is split into interleaved groups, and even then it is a η = 1
  ceiling. The realistic lead is 25.3k.
- **Revision 1 applied η only to continuous.** Its falsifier compared continuous at η 0.85 with
  lockstep at η 1. At equal η, continuous wins by 10–24 %.
- **"REPO-A shows the core's ping-pong is optimistic" was wrong.** REPO-A measured interval =
  max(Tp, Tc) (295 ns = max(100, 295)); 1.339× is serial/overlapped. The core's law is confirmed.
  What was missing was the second bank's area (now charged) and its capture error (sent to N6/N8).
- **Tile numbers were wrong.** The tile density is 3.30 TMAC/s/mm² of tile (2.17 per mm² of die),
  not 1.9, and the baseline's die density is 2.6× higher, not 2.7×. The tile is 421 fJ/MAC, not
  433.7, and the die holds 2,130 tiles (2,234 before the ping-pong area was charged), not 2,068.
- **The wire energy was 2× high:** 64 fJ/bit/mm became 32 (α01 = 0.25). Unicast e_in is
  166 fJ/B, not 376.
- **The exile did not charge the rail time.** It now costs −2.2 % tok/s instead of gaining
  +0.6 %.
- **Rail headroom was relative to a fixed 5.1 TMAC/s/mm².** It is now relative to each design's
  own demand. h 0.5 was optimal only at η = 1, and the optimum is 2 at η 0.85.
- **Calibration was "free in slack".** The tiles are about 99 % busy and the reload window is
  0.004 %, so calibration is now charged (−0.9 % per 1 %).
- **The "2.7× core overclaim under Sohu" was an undersized buffer.** At the best buffer the
  overclaim is 2.6 %.
