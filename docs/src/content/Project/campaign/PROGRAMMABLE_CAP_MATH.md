# Programmable-capacitor bypass: conditional charge and noise identities

**VERIFIED linear RC identities; SPECULATIVE transistor implementation.**
Root's physical control connects an unused capacitor's bottom plate back to
its own column instead of to a quiet reference. This can retain the active-C
signal law while paying installed capacitor area and programming switches.
The following analysis is independent of that forthcoming physical fixture.

Let the useful native column capacitance be Cs. An unused capacitor C joins
the column to a bottom node; its enabled bypass has resistance R between the
same nodes. Let Cp represent the bottom node's off-switch parasitic capacitance
to a quiet reference. With s the Laplace frequency, the column sees

```
Yunused(s) = s*Cp*(1+s*R*C)/(1+s*R*(C+Cp)).
```

Thus its low-frequency load is Cp, while the high-frequency limit is the
series capacitance C*Cp/(C+Cp). C itself does not become a direct grounded
column load. For Cp=0 the unused capacitor carries only an internal
differential mode. An off switch coupled to a moving row is not a quiet Cp:
its row-to-column feedthrough must be included in the physical test.

For several unused capacitors, let M be the complete capacitance matrix of
the column and their bottom nodes, and let one denote the all-ones vector.
With only their bypass resistors active, total conductor charge
`Q=one^T*M*v` is conserved. At one temperature, with fully thermalized
internal modes and fixed Q, the voltage covariance is

```
Sigma = kT*(M^-1 - one*one^T/(one^T*M*one)).
```

The first term is the unconstrained capacitor covariance. Conditioning on
total charge removes only the common-charge mode; it does not remove all
internal thermal motion. Schur elimination gives

```
Var(vcolumn) = kT*[1/(Cs+sum(Cj*Cpj/(Cj+Cpj)))
                  -1/(Cs+sum(Cpj))].
```

For Cs much larger than the summed bottom parasitics this is approximately
`kT*sum(Cpj^2/(Cj+Cpj))/Cs^2`. It tends to zero as parasitics vanish. A larger
unused capacitor can suppress its bypass differential-mode contribution,
despite still consuming installed area. This is a conditional passive-network
identity, not an assertion that MOS programming switches are noiseless.

The independent [checker](../../../../../build/campaign/weight_digits/bypass_noise_check.py)
passes256 fixed-seed matrix cases with1/2/7/32 unused capacitors. It compares
the closed-form column variance with full matrix inversion, verifies the
conserved-charge constraint, covariance positivity, and the continuous-time
Lyapunov equation generated by the resistor conductance matrix. Separately,
complex Schur elimination verifies the input-admittance formula. A zero-Cp
control confirms no column perturbation from the internal bypass mode.
The [result](../../../../../build/campaign/weight_digits/bypass_noise_check.json) retains
the checker hash, seed, scope and numerical residuals.

This covariance excludes reset-generated common-charge noise. If another
resistor resets the whole network, that common mode is excited as well and
must be restored with its correct phase covariance. Finite sharing/reset time,
measurement bandwidth, nonlinear MOS charge, switch transitions, leakage,
weight programming, row/reference coupling and mismatch remain unverified.
Installed area includes every unused physical capacitor and its switches.
The next physical checks are column admittance, row feedthrough, native radix
matching, full reset/share noise, and post-programming settling at fixed
geometry and operating conditions. No system noise credit is taken here.
