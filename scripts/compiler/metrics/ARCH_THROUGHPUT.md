N4/7B: t_in = 13.60 ns, conv = 94.86 ns, tau = 3.645 ns, tiles = 46,666, passes = 27,343,750

## 0. The scoreboard collapses to TWO terms, not three

`tiles_per_die` and `passes_per_token` are not independent: `passes_per_token = P_eff/(N*M)` and `tiles = A_die*fill/A_tile`, so

```
tok/s/die = (A_die * fill) * rho_W / (P_eff * pass_time)
   rho_W   = weights per mm2   (law:convdens numerator/denominator)
   P_eff   = weights ACTUALLY touched per token (dense P, or less)
```

So there are exactly three currencies: **weight density rho_W** (baseline 42,667 weights/mm2, _derived_ from the shipped `TILE_MM2['tsmc_n4_proj']`), **pass_time**, and **P_eff**.  Every lever below is one of the three.  This is `law:convdens` restated for tok/s, and it is why the paper's own line *'the architecture problem IS conversion minimization'* is the right frame. _(derived)_

`law:convdens` also gives the honest yardstick: the densest reported array in the paper's own table is **4.2e5 weights/mm2** (40 nm NOR flash). Our N4 mini tile is 42,667 weights/mm2 — **9.8x LESS dense than a 40 nm flash part**, at four nodes' advantage. That gap is worth more than every timing lever in this document combined, and it is entirely converter+C_int area. _(derived, from law:convdens)_

Resolve this before scoring any lever that buys time with area. The paper's own `law:convdens` premise (*'measured weight densities are set by converter pitch, not cells'*) says `phi -> 1` is the defensible prior, which is the pessimistic column throughout.

## 2. VERIFIED VERDICT — converter multiplexing S: the 8x is NOT real

`THROUGHPUT_CEILING.md` lever #6 ranks S first (*'S=8 gets window-bound with no K, no dB'*, *'up to 8x, the largest runway found'*). That ranking **priced the time and not the area**, even though the same row names `M*A_conv*S` as the cost. Pricing it:

**Sign warning.** The `S` in `law:convdens`'s `M*A_conv(B_y)/S` is a column *sharing* factor (27h4: S columns share one converter). The lever asked about is its **reciprocal** — S converters per column — so the denominator term becomes `M*A_conv*S`. The two S's are inverses.

```
time :  pass(S) = max(t_in, conv/S) + 4 t_q     -> saturates at conv/t_in = 7.0x
area :  A(S)    = A(1) * (1 - phi + S*phi)      phi = (conv+hold)/A_tile
gain :  tok/s(S)/tok/s(1) = min(S, 8.0) / (1 + (S-1)*phi)
```

`gain` is maximised at exactly `S = conv/t_in = 8` for any phi (past 8 the numerator freezes and the denominator keeps growing), which is the one part of the original claim that survives. _(derived)_

| S | pass_time | phi=0.11 (shipped area, S/H) | phi=0.17 (shipped area, dup C_int) | phi=0.66 (model area, S/H) | phi=0.98 (model area, dup C_int) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 95.3 ns | 1.00x | 1.00x | 1.00x | 1.00x |
| 2 | 47.8 ns | 1.80x | 1.71x | 1.21x | 1.01x |
| 4 | 24.1 ns | 2.99x | 2.65x | 1.35x | 1.01x |
| 8 | 14.0 ns | 3.89x | 3.19x | 1.25x | 0.89x |
| 16 | 14.0 ns | 2.58x | 1.97x | 0.64x | 0.44x |

phi values _(derived)_: converter 25 um2/col (paper converged) + either a duplicated C_int (14.9 um2/col) or an ISAAC-style S/H at the kT/C floor (C_h >= 12kT*4^8/V_FS^2 = 5.8 fF = 1.66 um2/col, 27h4).

**Verdict on S:**

- The **8x is time-only and does not survive area.** Best case (3.89x) needs BOTH the optimistic tile-area basis AND an S/H-based mux; worst case (0.89x) it is *exactly nothing*.
- **Do not duplicate C_int.** ISAAC's structure (one S/H per column at the kT/C floor, then an S:1 mux into S converters) costs 1.66 um2 instead of 14.9 um2 per extra path — a 9x area saving on the replicated element, and it is the difference between 1.0x and 1.4x on the pessimistic basis. The ping-pong C_int we already ship is the *expensive* way to do S=2. _(derived, literature: shafiee2016isaac, 27h4)_
- **27h4's warning binds physically.** Our converter is 25 um2 against a crosspoint pitch of ~0.16 um — the converter is already ~1008 crosspoints wide. *'The sharing factor is a measurement, not a decision'*: there is no room under the column pitch for a second converter, let alone eight. S=8 is a floorplan claim nobody has drawn.
- **Honest replacement for the #1 ranking:** S is worth **1.2x–3.9x**, not 8x, and only via S/H + mux, and only after section 1 is resolved.

## 3. Inter-layer / inter-tile pipelining: EXACTLY 1.000x. Here is the arithmetic.

At 7B a die holds 46,666 tiles x 256 weights = 11.9M weights, so the model is **R = 586x time-multiplexed** _(derived)_ — the ~600x in the brief.

Two ways to spend that R:

| scheme | per-die tok/s | token latency | weight writes/token/die |
|---|---|---|---|
| time-multiplex 1 die, R rounds | 17,915 | 55.8 us | 7e+09 |
| ISAAC-style spatial pipeline over R dies, model resident | 17,915 | 55.8 us | **0** |

```
multiplexed : tok/s/die = tiles*N*M / (P * pass)          = 1/(R*pass)
pipelined   : system retires 1 token per pass_time over R stages,
              so per die  = (1/pass)/R                    = 1/(R*pass)
              latency     = R*pass  (identical: same R serial passes)
```

**They are the same number, identically, for any R.** _(derived, asserted in the self-check.)* Inter-layer pipelining converts multiplexing depth into pipeline depth at constant tok/s/die and constant latency. There is no headroom in it, because the shipped formula **already assumes 100% tile utilisation** — `GATING_VALUE.md` says so in as many words: *'it assumes 100% tile utilisation, and at 7B/70B that assumption holds by a factor of ~600'*.

Nor is there a dependency stall to recover: batch-1 decode GEMV has 46,666-way *output-column* parallelism inside every single matmul, so tiles never wait on each other within a round. ISAAC's inter-layer pipeline exists to fill a CNN's *layer-shaped* bubbles; a decode GEMV has none. **DEAD as a throughput lever.**

### But pipelining is not worthless — it is the fix for a wall nobody priced

The last column above is the point. Time-multiplexing writes **every weight in the model into an array once per token**: at 17,915 tok/s that is **62.7 TB/s** of weight-write traffic into one die, and **427 TB/s** at the 121,905 tok/s ceiling. _(derived)_

| reference | bandwidth | our need at 15.5k tok/s | at the 122k ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| HBM3e, 1 stack | 1.2 TB/s | 52x short | 356x short |
| HBM3e, 8 stacks | 9.6 TB/s | 6.5x short | 44x short |

**The tok/s model silently assumes infinite weight-load bandwidth.** `PDK_PROJECTIONS.md` states the exclusion for *energy* (*'weights time-multiplexed (rewrite energy excluded)'*) and `GATING_VALUE.md` priced the energy side (77 fJ/write break-even). **Nobody priced the TIME side.** At 7B this machine is not weight-stationary — it is a 586x-reloaded array, i.e. exactly the memory wall `law:roofline` claims analog residency deletes. `law:roofline`'s `Q -> 0` is true only when the model FITS.

Three things fix it, and none of them is a throughput lever:
1. **Spatial pipelining over R=586 dies** (weights truly resident): traffic -> 0, tok/s/die unchanged. This is what pipelining is *for*.
2. **Batching**: 62.7 TB/s / B. At B=64, 0.98 TB/s — feasible. Free, because `law:batch` already charges analog `t(B)=B*t_MVM`, so tok/s/die is batch-invariant (section 5).
3. **Contextual sparsity / MoE** (section 4): only the active weights are ever loaded, so traffic falls by the same factor as passes.

## 4. THE BIGGEST UNSPENT LEVER: the measured accuracy budget has never been cashed

`specs.SNR_T_ATTN_DB = 28.0` dB is the target that makes K=1, and it is an **assumed** *'attention-class'* constant. Put it in the same units as the repo's two measured model-adequacy results (sigma_signal = CODE_MAX/4 = 30 codes, sigma_err = L/sqrt(3)):

| gate | +-LSB on the +-127 code | equivalent CSNR | provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| old converter-ENOB spec | 1 | 34.3 dB | tb gate; == `specs.SNR_S_DB` 34.0 dB (consistency check) |
| `SNR_T_ATTN_DB` in force | 2.1 | 28.0 dB | **ASSUMPTION** |
| measured tile budget (#24 ERROR_IMPACT) | 8 | 16.3 dB | **measured** (argmax 100%, cos 0.9948) |
| measured o_t budget (#26 OT_IMPACT) | 18.8 | 8.8 dB | **measured** (argmax 100%, cos 0.9935) |

**The target in force is 11.7 dB stricter than the repo's own measured tile budget.** That is ~2.0 bits of margin that has been paid for and never spent. It is **one** budget and it can be spent **one** of two ways:

### (a) spend it on K (deeper cascade)

`K = 10^((SNR_s - SNR_T)/10)`; the gain term is dropped because the parallel super-tile is SPICE-VERIFIED gain-error-K-independent (84410e6).

| SNR_s | SNR_T = 28.0 (in force) | SNR_T = 16.3 (#24 measured) |
|---|---|---|
| 28.5 dB | K=1 -> 17,915 tok/s (0.29x) | K=16 -> 121,905 tok/s (1.95x) |
| 19.9 dB | K=1 -> 17,915 tok/s (0.29x) | K=2 -> 35,681 tok/s (0.57x) |

### (b) spend it on B_y (coarser converter = shorter conversion)

`coarse_earlyexit.py`'s algebra generalised in B_y: `COARSE_CAP = (2^(B_y-1)-1) >> 4`, `n_coarse = cap + 4`, `conv = n_coarse*2*tau + 8*tau`. Be precise about how many bits are actually free: vs the +-1 LSB converter-ENOB spec, +-8 LSB is 3 bits; vs the 28.0 dB target in force (+-2.1 LSB) it is 2.0 bits. So **B_y 8->6 is fully covered by #24 alone**; 8->5 spends into the looser #26 o_t headroom and needs the depth check below.

| B_y | code error in B_y=8 LSBs | n_coarse | conv_time | tok/s | vs Sohu | + pipelined SAR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 1 | 9 | 94.9 ns | 17,915 | 0.29x | 25,852 (0.41x) |
| 7 | 2 | 5 | 65.7 ns | 25,819 | 0.41x | 46,310 (0.74x) |
| 6 | 4 | 3 | 51.1 ns | 33,127 | 0.53x | 76,628 (1.23x) |
| 5 | 8 | 2 | 43.8 ns | 38,588 | 0.62x | 113,920 (1.82x) |

The **pipelined SAR** column is a second, independent, near-free architectural lever: the 4-b fine SAR is 8 tau = 29.2 ns = 31% of the conversion and it is *sequential after* the coarse loop. Latch the residue onto a 6 fF hold cap and pass n+1's coarse loop overlaps pass n's SAR — a textbook 2-stage pipeline ADC. Cost: one small cap + one mux per column (1.66 um2/col, phi impact ~4.1%). Worth **1.44x on its own** at B_y=8, no accuracy cost at all. _(derived; NOT measured)_

**Combined cheapest path to the full runway** _(projected)_: B_y=5 + pipelined SAR gives conv = 14.6 ns; add S=2 (cheap, S/H) -> 7.3 ns against the 13.6 ns window floor = 121,905 tok/s = 1.95x Sohu — 6.8x of the 6.8x runway, with no K, no gm scaling, and ONE extra converter per column instead of eight. Folding in `COARSE_MARGIN` 4->2 (already flagged in `specs.py` as unmeasured and worth ~1.15x) clears the floor outright: 7.3 ns < 13.6 ns at the same S=2.

### The honest caveat that gates all of section 4

Both LSB budgets were measured on **one block of a 135M model** with a **proxy LM head** (ERROR_IMPACT.md says so itself). A 7B model is ~30 blocks deep. If per-block errors are independent and accumulate in the residual stream, the per-block budget shrinks by sqrt(30) -> +-1.5 LSB -> 31.0 dB, which is *stricter* than the 28.0 dB in force. If they are correlated / absorbed by RMSNorm, the +-8 stands and the whole runway opens.

> **The single highest-value experiment available: re-run the #24 error-injection sweep at full model depth.** It is pure numpy, no SPICE, and it is worth between 1.00x and 6.8x on tok/s — more than every circuit lever in `OPTIMIZATION_RESULTS.md` combined. Session 2's mandate was *'raise source SNRs'* (OTA, C_int, chopping); nobody asked whether the target needed to be that high.

Same idea from the outside _(literature)_: hardware-aware / noise-aware training reaches iso-accuracy with FP for transformers on analog IMC (Rasch et al., *Nat. Commun.* 2023; IBM/ETH *Analog Foundation Models*, 2025). That is a training-side purchase of the same dB, at zero silicon. It is absent from every session mandate in this repo.

## 5. Dead on arrival — and exactly why

| lever | why it is dead | gain |
|---|---|---|
| **Batch / weight replication** | `law:batch`: analog is strictly `t(B)=B*t_MVM`, so B tokens cost B passes. tok/s = B*tiles/(passes*B*pass). Cancels identically. | **1.000x** _(derived, asserted)_ |
| **Inter-layer pipelining** | already credited: the formula assumes 100% utilisation; pipelining just relabels R (section 3) | **1.000x** |
| **Speculative / multi-token decoding** | the GPU win is arbitrage on a memory wall: a batch-1 and a batch-g forward cost the same when bandwidth-bound. `law:batch` says analog cost is *linear* in the verified block, so you pay full price for rejected drafts **plus** the draft model. | **< 1.0x — NEGATIVE** _(derived)_ |
| **Medusa / lookahead / multi-token heads** | same mechanism, extra heads = extra passes | **< 1.0x** |
| **Bit-serial input encoding (27h5)** | attacks `t_in` only (13.6 ns), which `max()` ignores at K=1. And 27h5's own math pays **B_x conversions instead of 1** — it *multiplies* the binding term. Anti-optimal for a conversion-bound charge-domain column. | **0x, then negative** |
| **Anything else that shortens t_in** (b_x, faster t_q, PWM tricks) | not the max() term until K>=8 | **0x** (already in CONVTIME_SENSITIVITY 4d) |
| **Analog dataflow between tiles (27l3)** | it *is* `law:cascade` applied spatially: K stages without requantisation -> sigma*sqrt(K). Draws the **same** SNR budget K already draws. `OPTIMIZATION_RESULTS` §4: *'levers don't multiply: cascade-K, lattice thresholds, per-layer-K all draw the same SNR budget'*. | **not independent of K** |
| **Unstructured activation sparsity** | a pass covers N=16 inputs and is skippable only if ALL are zero: P = p^N. At p=0.9 that is 0.185; at N=512 it is 3.7e-24. **Value collapses exponentially in N** — the wider the column, the more worthless it gets. The paper's 88.9%/2304-row claim is about ADC *range*, not pass skipping. | **~1.2x at N=16, 1.00x at N=512** _(derived)_ |
| **Adaptive-range converter, CSD, DPS-48, larger N, higher clock, LVT, VDD gating** | already refuted in `OPTIMIZATION_RESULTS.md` | — |
| **CAM / top-k attention (27l9)** | attacks the KV-cache matmuls, which are **activations, not weights** — they are not in the 7e+09 weight count that sets `passes_per_token` at all. It is a Chip-2 latency lever, worth 0x on the Chip-1 weight-engine scoreboard. | **0x here** |
| **Output-stationary / different loop order** | batch-1 GEMV has no operand reuse to reorder; every weight is touched once. | **1.000x** |

## 6. Live levers on `P_eff` (passes_per_token) — and why WE get them cheap

`P_eff` is the number of weights actually touched per token. For MoE that is the **active** parameter count, so the comparison must be **quality-matched, not size-matched** — a 7B-total MoE with E/k=32 has 219M active params and is not a 7B-quality model. Tabulating real models by their active count removes the ambiguity _(projected)_:

| workload | P_eff (weights/token) | passes/token | tok/s | vs Sohu | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7B dense (baseline) | 7e+09 | 27,343,750 | 17,915 | 0.29x | reference |
| 70B dense | 7e+10 | 273,437,500 | 1,792 | 0.03x | reference (Sohu's own workload) |
| Mixtral 8x7B (47B total, k/E=2/8) | 1.29e+10 | 50,390,625 | 9,722 | 0.16x | ~70B-dense quality at 5.4x the tok/s of 70B dense (_literature_) |
| DeepSeek-V3-class (671B total, 37B active) | 3.7e+10 | 144,531,250 | 3,389 | 0.05x | frontier quality; 18x fewer passes than its dense equivalent |
| 7B dense + Deja Vu contextual sparsity (composite 0.30) | 2.1e+09 | 8,203,125 | 59,718 | 0.96x | same model, _literature_ predictor |
| 7B dense + MLP-only sparsity (85% MLP sparse) | 3.03e+09 | 11,848,958 | 41,343 | 0.66x | conservative, MLP only |

`law:moe`'s E/k shows up as the ratio between rows: Mixtral's 12.9B active vs 70B dense is 5.4x at matched quality, DeepSeek's 37B vs 671B is 18.1x. _(derived)_

Deja Vu (Liu et al., ICML 2023) _(literature)_: >95% of MLP parameters and >80% of attention heads are zeroable per token with no quality loss, via a lookahead predictor one block ahead; ProSparse / TurboSparse reach ~90% intrinsic activation sparsity in Llama-class models by ReLU-fication.

### The synergy nobody has written down

Structured sparsity needs the active neurons **gathered into dense tiles**, otherwise you are back to the `p^N` problem above. A truly weight-stationary analog machine cannot gather: its weights are burned into fixed arrays and a skipped column just idles.

**We are 586x time-multiplexed, so we CHOOSE which weights to load every round.** Gathering the Deja-Vu-predicted active neurons into dense tile loads is free for us and impossible for a resident array. The 586x multiplexing — the thing section 3 identifies as a 54 TB/s liability — is *exactly* what makes contextual sparsity convert 1:1 into passes AND into a 1:1 reduction of that same weight traffic. _(derived; this is the strongest architectural argument in the document.)*

This also collapses the MoE story into it: `law:moe`'s k/E is just contextual sparsity with a trained, block-structured predictor. Same mechanism, same hardware requirement (dynamic tile loading), and they **do not stack** — a MoE's experts are already the gathered active set.

## 8. From the literature: things this repo has NOT considered at all

1. **Extreme partial-sum quantization / near-ADC-less CiM** _(literature: HCiM arXiv:2403.13577; Kim et al., ACM JETC 2022 'Extreme Partial-Sum Quantization')_. Train the network so the *partial sum* — not the final activation — can be 1-1.5 bits, and the converter collapses to a sense amp. This repo has explored adaptive converter *range* (refuted, 0.2%) but never reduced converter *resolution*, which is what actually shortens conv_time (38,588 tok/s at B_y=5). It is the same budget as section 4, but the literature says training can *manufacture* the budget rather than spend existing slack.
2. **Hardware-aware / noise-aware training as an SNR purchase** _(literature: Rasch et al. Nat. Commun. 14:5282, 2023; IBM/ETH Analog Foundation Models 2025)_. Every session mandate in this repo buys dB with circuits (OTA current, C_int, chopping, bit-slicing, LVT). Nobody has tried buying dB with the *model*. It is the cheapest dB on the table and it composes with K directly.
3. **The weight-load bandwidth wall (section 3).** Not in the literature we found either — published analog-IMC LLM work assumes resident weights (IBM 64-core, HERMES, NeuRRAM all evaluate models that fit). At 7B/586x this is AnalogIOC-specific and it is unpriced. It does not change tok/s but it changes whether the tok/s number means anything.
4. **Tile-granular gather for contextual sparsity (section 6).** The IMC sparsity literature (STICKER-IM, CompRRAE, OU-based compression) all works on *resident* arrays and therefore has to skip in place, getting `p^N`. Nobody has pointed out that a time-multiplexed analog array gets structured sparsity at full 1:1 value. This is a claimable architectural contribution, not a borrowed one.

Checked and found already-covered/refuted here: ISAAC's S/H + fast shared ADC (= our ping-pong, and section 2's S/H recommendation); PUMA (instruction pipeline — a programmability result, not throughput); Newton MICRO'18's three techniques (adaptive ADC precision = refuted adaptive-range; divide-and-conquer numerics = refuted DPS-48/Strassen; heterogeneous provisioning = done, per-layer K); CASCADE/TIMELY (= 27l3 analog inter-tile dataflow, same SNR budget as K); PRIME (sense-amp reuse = trades resolution we do not have to spare).
