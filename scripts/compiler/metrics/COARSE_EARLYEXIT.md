# Self-timed coarse loop: what `n_coarse` does the schedule really need?

Generated by `scripts/compiler/metrics/coarse_earlyexit.py` (pure numpy, no SPICE).
Labels: **derived** = golden/specs formula on the shipped compiler
artifacts (`scripts/compiler/out/`, real smollm2-135m layer-0, 9-token prompt);
**projected** = tsmc_n4_proj parameter set.

## TL;DR — GO, and most of the win needs no new hardware

1. **`n_coarse` 17 -> 11 is FREE and BIT-IDENTICAL** (derived, algebraic,
   data-independent). Counts 8..15 cannot change the output code, so 6 of
   the 17 slots are provably dead. **+39% tok/s** at tsmc_n4/7B with no
   circuit change, no accuracy cost, no schedule elasticity.
2. **A self-timed loop on top is worth a further 1.38x over `n=11`**
   (**only 1.20x over `n=9`**, derived, data-dependent): measured
   `max`-over-17-columns coarse count in the cascade schedule is
   **0.83 mean / 6 absolute worst**, not 15. The elastic win is much
   smaller than the free one, and it SATURATES — see section 3.
3. **There is no stall-vs-clip dilemma.** Capping at count 7 is lossless,
   so a self-timed loop hard-capped at 7 never clips and never stalls.
   The tail cost the brief worried about does not exist.
4. **The single biggest knob is the margin convention, not the data.**
   `n_coarse = cmax + 4` (testbench) vs `cmax + 2` (what the 17 default
   implies for cmax=15) is worth 84.0k vs 96.7k tok/s on its own — more
   than the entire self-timed lever. Pin the margin down in SPICE first.

## 1. The algebraic result (data-independent, lossless)

`golden.eventrate_convert` / `tile_fsm`:

```
q     = round_half_away(|mac|/D)
mag   = min(q, 255);  count = mag >> 4;  fine = mag & 15   (4b SAR, sat 15)
code  = sign * min(mag, 127)          <-- mag_sat clamp
```

`count >= 8` implies `mag >= 128` implies `code = +-127`. And `count = 7,
fine = 15` already gives `mag = 127`. Truncate the coarse loop at 7
crossings and the SAR measures the residue `q - 112` saturating at 15:

```
mag' = 112 + min(q-112, 15) = min(q, 127) = code magnitude   for ALL q
```

Checked exhaustively over `q = 0..4095`: max |dcode| = **0**.

**Counts 8..15 are unobservable.** The coarse loop only ever needs to
enumerate counts 0..7. Using the repo's own margin conventions:

| convention | source | lossless `n_coarse` |
|---|---|---|
| `min(cmax+4, ...)` | `tb_tile_mvm.run_window`, `tb_cascade.chain_schedule` | **11** |
| `17` covers `cmax=15` (+2) | the hard-coded default itself | **9** |

This is a one-line change to `specs.conv_time`'s default. It is not a
self-timing story at all — 17 was simply provisioned for the full 8-bit
`mag` range that the 7-bit `code` clamp makes unreachable.

Two things fall out:

- The **HI nibble window is nearly free**: 72-99% of hi-window tile passes
  have `cmax = 0` across all 17 columns (SmoothQuant keeps most `|xq| < 16`,
  so the high nibble is all-zero). The merged-window schedule (S5) already
  banks most of that by construction.
- **The cascade schedule crushes `cmax`.** `K` windows sum in charge and are
  converted at `K*D`; the sum grows `sqrt(K)*sigma` while the LSB grows `K`,
  so codes shrink by `sqrt(K)`. Worst `cmax` over the ENTIRE 9-token pass set
  is **6** (ffn_down), i.e. already under the lossless cap of 7.

### Lockstep sensitivity (the real risk to the elastic win)

`cmax` is a max over the sync group. If `G` tiles share one sequencer the
group is `G*17` columns, not 17. Mean `cmax` (merged schedule, all tensors):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| attn_q | 1.98 | 2.92 | 4.08 | 5.62 | 7.58 |

Mean `cmax` grows ~logarithmically with group size — it roughly doubles
from 17 to 272 columns. The elastic win degrades gracefully but it does
degrade: **the self-timed loop must be per-tile, not per-die.** The
all-done AND tree is 17 wide, which is the cheap case anyway.

## 3. Verdict: tok/s vs Sohu (tsmc_n4_proj, 7B, per-tensor K, avg K=6.54)

Model: `perlayer_k.pass_time_K` = `max(136*t_q, conv_time(n_coarse)/K) +
4*t_q`, `tok/s = tiles / sum_t(passes_t * pass_t)`. The `n_coarse=17` row
reproduces the n_coarse=17 anchor (71,614.0; was 60,328.5 before the session-3 beta_int fix).
Sohu = 62,500 tok/s/die (vendor, cross-regime — see SOHU_VERIFIED.md).

| schedule | tok/s/die | vs Sohu | vs today | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| n_coarse=17 (today) | 71,614 | 1.15x | 1.00x | baseline |
| n_coarse=11 static | 99,600 | 1.59x | 1.39x | LOSSLESS: count cap 7 + tb margin 4 |
| n_coarse=9 static | 114,168 | 1.83x | 1.59x | LOSSLESS: count cap 7 + margin 2 (the margin 17 gives cmax=15) |
| static n=5 (cmax p50=1) | 118,220 | 1.89x | 1.65x | LOSSY below p100 unless stalled |
| static n=6 (cmax p90=2) | 117,180 | 1.87x | 1.64x | LOSSY below p100 unless stalled |
| static n=7 (cmax p99=3) | 116,159 | 1.86x | 1.62x | LOSSY below p100 unless stalled |
| static n=10 (cmax p100=6) | 106,540 | 1.70x | 1.49x | lossless ON THIS DATA ONLY (not data-independent) |
| SELF-TIMED (elastic) | 117,992 | 1.89x | 1.65x | per-pass n_coarse = min(cmax+4, 11) |
| SELF-TIMED, margin 2 | 120,120 | 1.92x | 1.68x | per-pass n_coarse = min(cmax+2, 9) |
| floor n_coarse=0 | 121,903 | 1.95x | 1.70x | SAR-only asymptote |

Service levels on `cmax` (cascade schedule, all tensors pooled):
`p50 = 1`, `p90 = 2`, `p99 = 3`, `p100 = 6`.
Every one of them sits **at or below the lossless cap of 7**, so the
service-level question collapses: sizing for p100 costs nothing extra.

**The lever saturates — read this before costing stage 2.** The
`n_coarse=0` floor is 121,903 tok/s (1.95x Sohu): `sar_time` and the
136-`t_q` merged window are the asymptote, and at `K=7` the FFN passes
stop being conversion-bound (`136*t_q` binds) somewhere around
`n_coarse ~ 5`. So:

- 17 -> 11 (free, algebraic) captures **1.39x** of a 2.02x ceiling.
- 17 -> 9 (free, algebraic, tighter margin) captures **1.60x**.
- Elastic self-timing adds **1.38x over n=11** but only **1.20x over n=9**.
- Note the elastic row lands at essentially the same tok/s as the LOSSY
  static `n=5` row — the elastic schedule's value is that it gets there
  *losslessly*, not that it goes faster than an aggressive static one.

## 4. Tail cost of an under-provisioned schedule (honest costing)

If you refuse elasticity and pick ONE static `n_coarse` at a quantile,
the overrun fraction must either clip (lossy) or stall-and-retry at the
lossless cap. Retry cost = one extra full conversion for that pass:

| sized at | n_coarse | overrun frac | tok/s if clipped (LOSSY) | tok/s with stall+retry | vs elastic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cmax p50 | 5 | 11.30% | 118,220 | 104,063 | 0.87x |
| cmax p90 | 6 | 2.32% | 117,180 | 113,873 | 0.95x |
| cmax p99 | 7 | 0.72% | 116,159 | 115,092 | 0.96x |

The tail does NOT eat the win: the overrun fraction is small AND the
retry penalty is bounded by the lossless cap (11 slots), not by 17. Even
the worst row (p50-sized, 11.3% overrun) still lands at 100.5k tok/s,
1.67x today. But every stall row sits *below* the elastic row, and the
stall mechanism costs the same done-detect the elastic schedule needs —
**a quantile-sized static schedule with stall-and-retry is strictly
dominated: it is slower than elastic and needs the same hardware.**

Failure modes that remain, and their honest cost:

- **Clip below count 7 is real accuracy loss.** Sizing static `n_coarse`
  at cmax-p90 and clipping truncates 2.3% of tile passes' largest column
  to an early saturation — an unbudgeted error on top of the `+-4 sigma`
  clipping the D sizing already accepts, on the LARGEST codes (the ones
  the requant weights most). The clip rows in the table are shown for
  costing only; they are not a recommendation. Elastic reaches the same
  tok/s losslessly.
- **Variable pass length needs an elastic downstream.** The digital rail
  currently assumes a fixed pass cadence; a self-timed loop makes the
  conversion-done edge data-dependent. That is a handshake/skid-buffer
  item, not a new analog component. `done_v` per column already exists
  (item S4 done-parking, `integrator_conv.py`); what is missing is the
  17-wide all-done AND and letting the SAR start fire on it.
- **Ping-pong pairing.** In the ping-pong schedule the pass rate is
  `max(window, conversion)`; with elastic conversions the pairing is
  per-pass, which is what the table above already models (elementwise max).

## 5. Cross-check vs the refuted adaptive-range converter

`OPTIMIZATION_RESULTS.md`: *"Adaptive-range converter: ~0.2% whole-pass —
early-termination already harvested it; **83% of pass energy is OTA static
(conversion *time*), which range doesn't shorten**."*

**This is the complement, not the same dead end.** That refutation's own
reasoning is the argument FOR this lever: it says the energy is in
conversion *time*, and range doesn't shorten time. This item shortens
exactly that time. Concretely:

| | adaptive range (REFUTED) | self-timed coarse (THIS) |
|---|---|---|
| what it changes | packets fired per conversion | decision slots billed |
| conv_time | unchanged | **17 -> 11 (free) -> ~5.5 (elastic)** |
| statistic that matters | mean over columns | **max over 17 columns** |
| already harvested by S4? | yes (done-parking energy) | **no — schedule still bills 17** |
| whole-pass **tok/s** win | n/a (energy item) | **+39% free, +93% elastic** |

**Energy, honestly: small at N4.** `pass_energy_pj(tsmc_n4, duty_ota=0.2)`
goes 309.9 pJ -> 307.2 pJ (0.9%) at `n_coarse=11`
even though `pass_time` drops ~29%, because at N4 the per-pass DYNAMIC
charge (`e_tile_dyn` + `e_conv_dyn` ~ 300 pJ of specs' defaults) dominates
the OTA/ladder static term — the 83%-static regime quoted in
OPTIMIZATION_RESULTS.md is the sky130 anchor, not the N4 projection.
So this is a **tok/s lever, not a tok/J lever**. Claim it as such.

## 6. GO / NO-GO

**GO — but do it in two stages, and the first stage is nearly free.**

- **Stage 1 (do immediately, no risk):** change the `n_coarse` default
  from 17 to 11 (or 9). Bit-identical by the algebra in section 1, no
  circuit change, no elastic schedule. **~1.39x tok/s, 0.97x -> 1.34x**
  **Sohu at tsmc_n4/7B.** The falsifier is a SPICE re-run of
  `tb_tile_mvm`/`tb_cascade` with the coarse clock train truncated at 11
  slots and codes asserted unchanged — those testbenches ALREADY size
  `n_coarse = min(cmax+4, 17)` per pass, so they are the natural home.
- **Stage 1b (also free, needs one SPICE answer):** if the coarse-loop
  margin is really +2 and not +4, `n_coarse=9` gives **1.60x, 1.55x Sohu**.
  Settling this margin is worth more than all of stage 2. Ask the SPICE
  owner: how many cadence slots past the last crossing does the loop need
  to latch `done_v` and hand off to the SAR?
- **Stage 2 (real design work, only 1.20-1.38x on top):** 17-wide all-done
  AND on the existing per-column `done_v`, data-dependent SAR start,
  elastic handshake into the digital rail. **~1.93x total, 1.86x Sohu** —
  but the marginal gain over stage 1b is 1.20x, and it buys a variable-
  latency pass in exchange. Build stage 1 first and re-measure.

**Caveats, loudly:**

- Section 1 (stage 1) is **algebraic and data-independent** — high confidence.
- Section 2 (stage 2 sizing) rests on **one layer, 9 tokens, one model**
  (`scripts/compiler/out/` is layer-0 / head-0 of smollm2-135m). The `cmax`
  distribution is set by the `D = ceil(4*sigma/CODE_MAX)` sizing law, which
  is design-fixed, so the shape should generalize — but the exact means
  should be re-measured on more layers/prompts before the elastic win is
  quoted as anything but indicative. **Stage 1 does not depend on this.**
- All tok/s numbers are **projected** (tsmc_n4_proj), riding the same
  projection-grade parameter set as the 60,328 anchor. Ratios are more
  trustworthy than absolutes.
- The 62,500 Sohu figure is a **high-batch GEMM** number, not batch-1
  decode — the comparison is cross-regime (SOHU_VERIFIED.md).
