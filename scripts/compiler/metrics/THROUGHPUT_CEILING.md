# THROUGHPUT CEILING — can f_MVM reach 1 GHz, and can we beat a systolic array?

Generated by `scripts/compiler/metrics/throughput_ceiling.py`. **No SPICE.** Pure parameter evaluation through `analog/schematics/specs.py` with the `pdk_projections.evaluate` runtime substitutions (per-PDK `TQ_SIM`; `sar_time` scaled by the `tau_absorb` ratio — forgetting the second one inflates N4 `conv_time` ~3x).

Labels: **measured** = SPICE-backed; **derived** = arithmetic on measured/committed values; **projected** = model extrapolation; **ASSUMPTION** = a named constant at the top of the script that a reader must supply or reject.

## Corrections to the framing, before any answer

Three numbers in the question do not survive checking. Stating them first, because two of the six answers change sign if they are kept.

1. **"tile = 512x256 MACs in ~0.006 mm2" — no.** `0.006 mm2` is `pdk_projections.TILE_MM2['tsmc_n4_proj']`, and that table is paired with `passes_per_token = 7e9/256`, i.e. **256 MACs/pass** — it is the **16x16 MINI tile**, 512x smaller than a 512x256 array. The paper's own 512x256 tile is 0.03-0.04 mm2 converged / 0.1 mm2 conservative (`sec_eval.tex`). This model gets 0.052 mm2 — larger than the paper, because the +-4 sigma swing law makes C_int (295 fF/column = 84 um2) the **largest single item in the tile at N=512**, bigger than the crosspoint array itself. Using 0.006 mm2 for a 512x256 tile inflates TOPS/mm2 by ~9x. _(derived)_
2. **"t_q floor ~75-90 ps at N4" — 92-97 ps, and only at M=17.** The floor is `5*R_row*C_row` on a wire that crosses M COLUMNS, so it goes as M^2. At the 256-column tile the question is about, an unsegmented row gives **22 ns**, not 92 ps (Q3, last table). _(derived)_
3. **"0.8-5.7 TOPS/tile" silently assumes tau_absorb is N-independent.** It is not: all N banks land on the OTA virtual ground, so `tau ~ N`. Without buying the time back with OTA current, the honest N=512 number is 0.29 TOPS/tile, not 2.75. Q3 is the whole argument. _(derived)_

## Q1 — What sets f_MVM, and its hard floor

```
pass(K) = max( T_in , T_conv/K ) + 4 t_q      f_MVM = 1/pass
T_in    = 136 t_q   (128-cycle merged PWM window + 8 settle-in)
T_conv  = n_coarse * 2*tau_absorb + t_SAR  =  26 * tau_absorb
           (n_coarse=11, cadence = K_SETTLE*tau = 2 tau, t_SAR = 240ns * tau/30ns = 8 tau)
```
At N4, t_q = 100 ps: T_in = **13.60 ns**, tau_absorb = 3.645 ns, T_conv = **94.86 ns** _(derived)_.

- **K=1 (the only K the measured 28.5 dB CSNR supports): CONVERSION binds**, by 7.0x. f_MVM = 10.5 MHz _(projected)_.
- **K -> inf: the PWM WINDOW binds**, hard. pass -> 140 t_q. f_MVM -> 71.4 MHz at t_q = 100 ps _(projected)_.
- Flip point: K = T_conv/T_in = **7.0** (i.e. K >= 9). Past that, conv_time work buys exactly zero _(derived)_.

### Is 1 GHz reachable? **No — by 14x, and the blocker is a WIRE, not a transistor.**

1 GHz => pass = 1 ns = 140 t_q => **t_q = 7.14 ps** _(derived)_. The user's 7 ps estimate is right.

`specs.t_q_floor` is `max(5*R_row*C_row, jitter_budget)` — a ROW-WIRE distributed-RC transit, not a device delay:

| PDK | row RC (5RC, M=17) | jitter budget | t_q floor | 1 GHz needs | short by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sky130 | 8.9 ps | 200 ps | **200 ps** | 7.1 ps | **28x** |
| asap7_proj | 91.7 ps | 50 ps | **92 ps** | 7.1 ps | **13x** |
| tsmc_n4_proj | 97.1 ps | 50 ps | **97 ps** | 7.1 ps | **14x** |

Two things that kill the 1 GHz idea outright _(derived)_:

1. **t_q is a BEOL RC transit, so a faster transistor does not touch it.** The 7.1 ps target is 14x below the N4 floor. There is no bias current, no topology and no device that buys it back.
2. **Row RC ANTI-SCALES with the node.** sky130 (130 nm, fat slow metal) 5RC = 8.9 ps; N4 97 ps — **11x WORSE at the newer node**, because r_sq rises (3.0 vs 0.125 ohm/sq) faster than the pitch and C_u fall. Shrinking the process to chase 1 GHz moves t_q the wrong way.

**Honest speed ceiling of this architecture (K -> inf, t_q at the true row-RC floor, M=17): f_MVM_max = 1/(140 * 97 ps) = 73.5 MHz** _(projected)_ — **13.6x short of 1 GHz**, and that already assumes a K the CSNR does not support. At the running t_q = 100 ps the ceiling is 71.4 MHz.

## Q2 — f_MVM is NOT the systolic clock. The comparison is not like-for-like.

TPUv1 MXU (256x256, 28 nm) at 940 MHz _(vendor/published (jouppi2017datacenter))_ retires **one MAC per PE per cycle**. Our f_MVM retires a **complete 512x256 MVM** — 262,144 ops per tick vs 131,072. Putting 11 MHz next to 940 MHz is a category error: the units are MVMs/s on one side and MACs/PE/s on the other. Do it properly, three scoreboards:

| scoreboard | when it is the right one | systolic 256x256 @940 MHz | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **peak TOPS** | batched GEMM, weights resident, B >> 256 | **123 TOPS** | marketing number; needs B >> 256 to approach |
| **batch-1 GEMV latency** | decode | 546 ns (513 cycles: 256 skew-in + 256 drain + 1) | fill/drain is the whole cost |
| **batch-1 effective MACs/s** | decode | **0.24 TOPS** (util **0.19%**) | 513x below peak |
| + weight load (256 cyc/new tile) | decode, model >> array | **0.16 TOPS** | every tile is new at 7B/batch-1 |

**Which scoreboard for which regime** (`law:roofline`, `law:batch`):

- `law:roofline`: arithmetic intensity is `I_w = B/b_w` — *dimensions cancel*, so enlarging the layer cannot rescue a small batch. At B=1 the digital machine is BANDWIDTH-bound and its peak TOPS is not the scoreboard at all; `Q/beta` is. Analog residency sends `Q -> 0`. **Right scoreboard for decode: MVM latency and tok/s, not TOPS.**
- `law:batch`: analog stationary-weight compute has strictly linear batch cost `t(B) = B t_MVM`; the digital weight-traffic term falls as 1/B. As B rises the comparison collapses to pure energy/op and area/op at matched precision — *both decided by the converter*. **Right scoreboard for batched GEMM: TOPS/mm2 and TOPS/W, and analog gets no batch economy to help it.**

Our side, same three (N=512, M=256, K=1, OTA current scaled to hold tau — see Q3): peak = batch-1 = **2.75 TOPS**, MVM latency **95 ns** for 262,144 ops _(projected)_. Against the systolic that is **0.022x on peak** and **11.5x on batch-1** — the *entire* sign flip is the systolic's 0.2% fill/drain utilisation, not our clock.

## Q3 — Raise N, not frequency. Where the N win actually stops.

### The premise is right, and two of the three laws do exit

- `law:mac` / Kirchhoff: the column accumulates all N rows in **one settling event**, so T_in and T_conv contain no N. `TOPS = 2 N M f_MVM` with f_MVM nominally N-free. **Correct.**
- `law:bout`: B_y ~ 8 **independent of N** — the dot product concentrates to sqrt(N), so clipping at +-4 sigma keeps the converter at 8 b forever. The converter does NOT grow with N. **Correct, and it is the key enabler.**
- `law:ir`: verbatim from `sec_laws.tex` — *"Charge-domain columns pass no DC and exit this law entirely"* (cites chen2021capram, bhardwaj2025toward). The N ~ 64-512 pin on resistive IMC **does not apply to us**. **Confirmed from the paper text.**
- `27g1`: the 19.6 dB / N<=125 knee is a CHARGE-SUMMING bitline-headroom effect. Its own *When it breaks* #2: *"Charge redistribution has no headroom term. In the QR compute model sigma_eta_h^2 = 0, so SNR_a does not collapse with N."* We are charge-redistribution onto a virtual-ground integrator. **The 125-row knee is not our knee.**

### But there is a fourth constraint the question did not list, and it is the one that binds

`analog/schematics/components/weight_tile/weight_tile.py`, verbatim:

> *"Column rails are the integrator virtual grounds (OTA inn nodes)"* ... *"TG to the column rail during phi2 (transfer)"*

So **all N crosspoint banks land on the OTA virtual ground during the very phi2 settling event `tau_absorb` measures**. `C_par_vg` is LINEAR in N: 3.75 fF/row at N4 (4 fF top ballast cap-shrink-scaled + 15 C_u), on a 20 fF fixed C_RAIL/wire base _(derived from the committed _CAL value at N=16)_.

Closed form, from `specs.tau_absorb` with `beta = C_int/(C_int+C_par)`:

```
tau_absorb * gm_in = A + A*C_par(N)/C_int(N) + C_par(N),   A = C_filt + C_ota_self
C_par(N) ~ N   and   C_int(N) ~ sqrt(N)  =>  tau ~ N   =>  T_conv ~ N
=> conversion-bound TOPS = 2*N*M/T_conv(N)  ->  CONSTANT in N.
```

**The N win and the OTA-loading loss are the same order and cancel.** That is the honest answer to Q3 and it is not what the note expected.

### Swing / charge headroom — the anchor-free number

With C_int AND C_u both held (just add rows): the +-4 sigma full scale grows as sqrt(N) and must stay under the **measured** OTA compression ceiling `MAC_MAX = 185` from the `CODE_MAX = 120` design point:

> **N_max / N_0 = (MAC_MAX/CODE_MAX)^2 = (185/120)^2 = 2.38x** _(derived, anchor-free)_

54% of spare headroom buys **2.4x of rows and nothing more**, because headroom is linear in sigma and N enters as sqrt(N). To go 8x in N one of C_int or C_u must move, and each has a price:

| route | what moves | price | binds at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** `cu` | C_u ~ 1/sqrt(N), C_int fixed | Pelgrom `sigma_C/C ~ C_u^-1/2 ~ N^1/4` => **-1.5 dB CSNR per octave of N** | CSNR |
| **B** `cint` | C_int ~ sqrt(N), C_u fixed | MOM area ~sqrt(N)/column **and** tau_absorb ~ N | area + conv_time |
| C | neither | clipping | 2.4x, hard |

**Route A max N** (solve `CSNR - 5 log10(N/N_0) >= 28.0`), from N_0 = 512:

- at the **measured 28.5 dB**: N <= **645** (1.26x) — 0.5 dB of margin is **a quarter of an octave**. _(projected)_
- at a hypothetical **34.0 dB**: N <= **8115** (15.8x). _(projected)_

**Read-off _(projected)_.** N = 512 -> 4096 is 8x the MACs and buys **1.19x TOPS** at K=1 — the OTA virtual-ground load eats 85% of it. The two things that DO improve monotonically are the ones `law:convdens` cares about: conversion energy per MAC (3.03 -> 0.38 fJ/MAC, c = 1/N) and converter-area amortisation. **TOPS/tile is not one of them.**

### The only way N buys TOPS: pay for it in static power

tau = C_out_eff/(beta*gm_in) and gm_in ~ I_side. Hold tau at the N=16 value by scaling the OTA current with the load:

| N | gm/current multiple | TOPS/tile | tile OTA static | TOPS/W (static only) | area mm2 | TOPS/mm2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512 | 9.5x | **2.75** | 21.8 mW | 126 | 0.052 | 53 |
| 1024 | 17.5x | **5.51** | 40.4 mW | 136 | 0.075 | 74 |
| 2048 | 33.2x | **11.02** | 76.5 mW | 144 | 0.113 | 97 |
| 4096 | 63.9x | **22.03** | 147.3 mW | 150 | 0.179 | 123 |

**TOPS ~ N, power ~ N, area ~ N => TOPS/W and TOPS/mm2 are FLAT.** N is a *scale* knob (buy more silicon, get more throughput), not an *efficiency* knob — exactly like adding PEs to a systolic array. The one thing it genuinely improves is `c = conversions/MAC = 1/N`, which is the `law:convdens` objective. That is real but it is a J/MAC win, not a TOPS win.

### Wire-RC feedback: it is on M, not N — and it is brutal

A row wire crosses COLUMNS. `specs.t_q_floor` has `R ~ M` and `C ~ M`, so **t_q_floor ~ M^2 and contains no N at all.** Growing N is free in t_q; growing M is quadratic:

| M (columns on one row wire) | unsegmented 5RC | with 1 re-driver per 17 cols | 
|---|---|---|
| 17 | 97 ps | 97 ps |
| 64 | 1,377 ps | 86 ps |
| 128 | 5,507 ps | 86 ps |
| 256 | 22,030 ps | 98 ps |
| 512 | 88,120 ps | 98 ps |

**Loud correction to the paper's own design point.** `sec_eval.tex` states t_q = 200 ps at N=512, M=256. The row-RC model in `specs.py` gives **22.0 ns** for an unsegmented 256-column row at N4 (and 2.0 ns even on sky130's fat metal) — **110x** the assumed value. The 512x256 tile is only buildable at 100 ps as ~15 locally re-driven row segments of ~17 columns. That is an architectural requirement, not a layout detail, and the re-drivers re-enter as edge skew (`sec_circuits`: *"Edge skew across 512 rows enters as input error"*). _(derived; flagged for the paper)_

## Q5 — Matched-PDK comparison. The 28 nm confound is REAL, and it flatters us.

The 940 MHz 256x256 reference is **28 nm** (vendor/published (jouppi2017datacenter)); our projection is N4. Three nodes of confound. Node-scaling law used (all **ASSUMPTION**, stated so it can be rejected):

| axis | systolic / digital | AnalogIOC | direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| frequency | `f ~ 1/FO4(node)`; FO4 12.5 -> 5.0 ps => **2.5x** | `f_MVM` has NO logic-delay term. t_q = row-wire RC, and `r_sq` RISES faster than pitch and C_u fall: sky130 8.9 ps -> N4 97 ps = **11x WORSE** (conv_time DOES improve: tau_absorb 25.1 -> 3.65 ns = 6.9x better) | **against us** |
| energy | `E ~ C V^2` improves with the node | `law:esnr`: the conversion floor is `12 kT 4^B_y` and *"supply voltage cancels exactly, so process scaling does not help at fixed resolution"*. `27g1`: SNR_a FALLS 65 -> 7 nm; energy at fixed SNR_pre is *higher* at 11/7 nm than at 22 nm | **against us** |
| area | `A_MAC ~ F^2` => **49x** 28 -> 4 nm | the crosspoint is a BEOL cap sized by MATCHING (`sigma_C/C = A_C/sqrt(area)`), not by F. SERVO_EG.md per-cell scatter 5.5% (sky130) -> 9.4% (N4) at the drawn C_u | **against us** |

> **All three node-scaling laws favour the systolic array.** Answering the user's question honestly *hurts* our case: comparing our N4 projection to a 28 nm TPUv1 was the flattering direction, not the unfair one.

**(a) Our architecture at a 28 nm-class node.** I will not interpolate. `specs.py` re-derives from `r_sq_wire`, `wire_pitch`, `vdd`, `cap_density_mim`, `c_int_col`, `jitter_budget_s` and a gm/ID table; there is no 28 nm `PDKConfig` in `library/pdks/` (sky130=130 nm, asap7=7 nm, tsmc_n4=4/5 nm) and the gm/ID tables are sky130-only (`specs.ota` widths are meaningless off sky130 — flagged in PDK_PROJECTIONS.md's own O1b list). What CAN be said without inventing constants: the row-RC term would land **between** sky130's 8.9 ps and asap7's 92 ps, so t_q at 28 nm would be **jitter-bound, not RC-bound** — i.e. 28 nm is a *better* node for our t_q than N4 is. _(derived from the two bracketing PDKs; the 28 nm point itself is NOT computed.)_

**(b) A systolic array at N4.** Framework only, constants named:

```python
SYS_N4 = {'rows': ?, 'cols': ?, 'f_hz': ?}   # fill from the lit agent
peak_tops = 2 * rows * cols * f_hz
b1_tops   = 2 * rows * cols / ((rows + cols + 1) / f_hz)
# our matched point is already at N4, so ONLY these three move.
```

Sensitivity, so the answer is known before the constants arrive: our best N4 point is **2.75 TOPS/tile**. A systolic array reaches that on peak with `rows*cols*f = 1.38e+12` — e.g. 256x256 at 21 MHz. **Any plausible N4 systolic beats us on peak TOPS by 1-2 orders of magnitude.** The crossover is on batch-1 utilisation and on J/MAC, and neither depends on the constants we are waiting for.

## Q6 — Verdict, per regime

### (i) Batched GEMM — **NO, by construction.**

`law:batch`, stated exactly: *analog stationary-weight compute has strictly linear batch cost `t(B) = B t_MVM` per tile or B x replicated tiles; a digital machine's weight-traffic term falls as 1/B*. **Analog gets no batch economy.** As B rises the digital array walks right along the roofline until it is compute-bound at its peak; we stay at `B * t_MVM`. Numerically:

- ours, best N4 point: **2.75 TOPS/tile** (N=512, M=256, K=1, gm ~ N) _(projected)_
- ours, window-bound ceiling (K -> inf, needs 37.6 dB CSNR we do not have): 19.3 TOPS/tile _(projected, NOT reachable at the measured CSNR)_
- systolic 256x256 @940 MHz: **123 TOPS** _(vendor)_ -> we are **45x short**, and 6x short even at the unreachable ceiling
- and every node-scaling law (Q5) moves that gap **wider**, not narrower.

**Large N does NOT flip this.** It cannot, for two independent reasons: (a) at K=1 the OTA virtual-ground load makes `T_conv ~ N`, so the 8x cancels to 1.19x; (b) buying the time back with `gm ~ N` gives TOPS ~ N at power ~ N and area ~ N — flat TOPS/W and flat TOPS/mm2, which is exactly what adding PEs does for the systolic array too. There is no structural advantage in the batched-GEMM regime. **Say it plainly: we lose (i), by construction, and no amount of N changes that.**

### (ii) Batch-1 decode GEMV — **YES, and it is not close.**

| | systolic 256x256 @940 MHz | AnalogIOC N4 (N=512, M=256, K=1) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| MVM latency | 546 ns for 131,072 ops | 95 ns for 262,144 ops | **5.7x** |
| effective TOPS | 0.24 | 2.75 | **11.5x** |
| + weight load | 0.16 | 2.75 (weights RESIDENT, Q -> 0) | **17.2x** |
| utilisation | **0.19%** | **100%** (one pass = one complete MVM, no fill, no drain) | 513x |

The mechanism is entirely the systolic array's own pipeline: 256+256+1 = 513 cycles of fill and drain to retire one GEMV. And `law:roofline` says the real batch-1 limit is not even that: at `I_w = B/b_w = 1` the digital machine is BANDWIDTH-bound (`Q/beta`), and *dimensions cancel* — a bigger model does not help. Analog residency deletes `Q`. **This is where the architecture is supposed to win, and it does.**

### The caveat that outranks all of it

The **28.5 dB** held-out CSNR is from **nominal SPICE** — `tb_csnr.py` says so in its own docstring: *"the tt corner carries no mismatch, and the tile's caps are ideal C elements anyway"*. The Pelgrom per-cell unit-cap scatter is an **additive, un-servoable** term (SERVO_EG.md 1(c)) worth 25.2 dB on its own at sky130's C_u and 20.5 dB at N4's. Powers add:

- sky130 (C_u = 0.15 fF): 28.5 dB (+) 25.2 dB Pelgrom = **23.5 dB** vs the 28.0 dB target -> **FAIL by 4.5 dB**
- asap7_proj (C_u = 0.10 fF): 28.5 dB (+) 23.5 dB Pelgrom = **22.3 dB** vs the 28.0 dB target -> **FAIL by 5.7 dB**
- tsmc_n4_proj (C_u = 0.09 fF): 28.5 dB (+) 20.5 dB Pelgrom = **19.9 dB** vs the 28.0 dB target -> **FAIL by 8.1 dB**

**So even K=1 is not established at the advanced nodes**, and route A (shrink C_u to grow N) makes it strictly worse. Any large-N plan must go via route B (grow C_int), which is the one that pays in tau_absorb.

## SPICE measurements this analysis needs (specified, not run)

1. **`tau_absorb` vs virtual-ground load.** Sweep the C_par_vg lumped cap on the OTA inn node over 80 fF / 320 fF / 1.28 pF / 5.1 pF (= N = 16/64/256/1024 at the derived 3.75 fF/row) and measure tau_absorb for each. **This single sweep decides Q3.** If tau grows sub-linearly the N verdict flips positive; the model says linear.
2. **Monte-Carlo Pelgrom on `tb_csnr uncorr`.** Inject `sigma_C/C = A_C/sqrt(C_u/rho)` per unit cap (A_C = 1.0-1.5 %*um), 100 seeds, re-score attention CSNR. Decides whether 28.5 dB survives at all — see the last section.
3. **`MAC_MAX` at raised V_SWING.** MAC_MAX = 185 is measured at the current swing; the 2.38x N ceiling and the whole route-B C_int scaling hang on the OTA staying LINEAR there. `tb_ota` swing sweep.
4. **Row-wire t_q with re-drivers.** A 256-column row segmented 16-ways with local re-drivers: does the edge land inside the 100 ps grid, and what is the segment-to-segment skew? Blocks the 512x256 tile entirely.
